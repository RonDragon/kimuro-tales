About Ringkit

Ringkit is a tiny lemur kit with soft grey-white fur, enormous round yellow eyes that glow faintly in the dark, and a fluffy tail already striped in black-and-white rings. It clings to cave walls with remarkable grip and peeks around corners at everything before committing to a direction. In the Shadow Caves it has learned to read the echo of its own tiny squeaks to navigate in total darkness - a skill it discovered independently at around three weeks old. Guardians say a Ringkit in the cave means the psychic fields here are stable enough for sensitive minds to enter safely.

Field note: Ringkit maps an entire cave system using echo-squeaks within its first hour, and can navigate it in total darkness from then on.

About Ringtail

Ringtail has grown upright, moving on two legs with a long striped tail held high for balance. Its eyes are large and luminous, capable of adjusting between complete darkness and sudden bright light in less than a heartbeat. It patrols the mid-caves of the Shadow Caves with quick precise movements, touching the walls with its paws at regular intervals as if checking something it cannot quite explain. It has developed a mild psychic ability to sense where other minds are, which it uses mostly to avoid crowds - though it will cross an entire cave to find a child sitting alone in the dark.

Field note: Ringtail can sense a lonely mind from fifty meters away and always approaches with the exact object that creature most needs to see.

About Lemurlux

Lemurlux is a striking creature - white-furred with luminous markings, its magnificent spiral tail swirling with living purple psychic energy. Its eyes glow gold-amber and seem to look slightly past the visible world. In the deepest Shadow Caves it floats a few centimeters off the ground, leaving no footprints. It can project calm directly into the minds of panicking creatures with a single glance, a gift the guardians relied upon when Abalont's shadow-storms made the caves a chaos of terrified minds. Its tail-spiral is different every time it moves - no two observers have ever drawn the same pattern.

Field note: Lemurlux's tail-spiral has never repeated the same pattern twice in recorded observations - scholars have been studying this for forty years.
