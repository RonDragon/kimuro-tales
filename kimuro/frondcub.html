About Frondcub

Frondcub is a round-bodied little grazer with stubby legs, a broad flat nose, and a cluster of fresh green leaves sprouting from the top of its head like a hat that grew there. Its hide is a warm sandy brown with faint scale-like patterning, and its wide dark eyes blink slowly as it samples every plant it encounters. It lives on the grassy ledges of Granite Heights where thin soil patches between the stone, and it has learned to eat every edible thing it finds immediately, because the next patch may be far away.

Field note: Frondcub can strip every edible leaf from a granite-ledge plant in under four seconds while leaving the roots and stem completely undamaged.

About Frondaur

Frondaur is a stocky, low-slung beast with rocky stone-plated armor across its back and a row of leaf-edged spines running from neck to tail. Thick clawed legs grip the granite paths effortlessly, and its broad snout is reinforced with dense cartilage for digging roots from rock crevices. It navigates the heights with slow, methodical confidence - never rushing, never retreating. Climbers say Frondaur's tunnels through dense undergrowth have saved more lives than any trail marker, because they always lead to the next ledge above.

Field note: Frondaur sheds exactly two stone-plates from its back each autumn; mountain builders collect them for steps because each one is load-bearing flat.

About Frondbara

Frondbara is a massive, shaggy-maned beast whose back is covered in thick stone-like armor plates interspersed with broad leaf-growths, as though the granite heights themselves have taken on animal form. Its great broad head sweeps low to the ground as it walks, and each footfall leaves a shallow impression in even the hardest ledge-stone. Lichen and small ferns grow from the older plates on its back, turning it into a walking garden that seeds the higher, barren ledges wherever it roams. Granite Heights settlers believe a mountain with a Frondbara on it will never lose its soil.

Field note: Frondbara has been observed carrying a fallen climber's pack for four hours up a sheer path to where help was waiting, without anyone having asked it to.
