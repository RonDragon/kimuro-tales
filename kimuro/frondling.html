About Frondling

Frondling is a small, lively fox-type creature with green-tipped leaf-ears and a bushy tail of layered leaves that holds warmth in the cool desert nights. Its green and tan coat blends into the dry scrub of the Amber Desert perfectly, and its small golden eyes catch firelight well in the pre-dawn hours when it does most of its exploring. It finds the sparse flowering plants of the desert fringes and curls around them to share warmth, keeping them alive through nights cold enough to kill most blooms.

Field note: Frondling's leaf-tail retains enough heat after a day in the desert sun to keep a small campfire-stone warm for three hours after sunset.

About Frondance

Frondance is a lean, swift fox-type creature whose layered leaf-mane fans out behind it as it runs the Amber Desert's dune-ridges. The undersides of its leaves have charred slightly golden from seasons of desert heat, giving its mane a warm amber-green color. It is a tireless runner, loping across ten kilometers of sand without breaking stride, and it can find the cool-side of any dune by scent alone. Desert caravans watch for Frondance's path: where it has recently run, the sand is firm enough to carry a loaded cart.

Field note: Frondance's paw-prints, when arranged in a line, point unerringly toward the nearest living plant with water in its roots.

About Frondelk

Frondelk is a powerful, broad-shouldered creature with a magnificent mane of large pointed leaves that range from deep green at the roots to warm amber-gold at the tips, as though caught permanently in sunset. Multiple tails of layered leaves fan behind it, and every step it takes on the dune-crest leaves a small ring of green shoots in the compacted sand that - impossibly - survive a full week before the desert heat claims them. The Amber Desert's old traders named its regular route the Green Road, and they navigate by it even when wind erases every other landmark.

Field note: Wherever Frondelk has slept, the sand underneath reveals a perfectly preserved grass-circle that dune scholars use to map ancient water-tables.
