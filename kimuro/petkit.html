About Petkit

Petkit is a small woolly four-legged creature with a round pale tan body and a single small nub of a horn on its forehead. Its fleece is fluffy and cream-colored, and its wide brown eyes are full of bright curiosity. It bounces from rock to rock in the amber desert, testing each stone with a quick headbutt before deciding whether to trust it. Young trainers in the region first meet a Petkit when it steals a memory-tablet off a low boulder and carries it proudly back to its herd. The nub-horn glows faintly orange when it finds a stone worth remembering.

Field note: Petkit headbutts the same boulder each morning - researchers have counted up to forty-three consecutive taps before it gives up.

About Petrocorn

Petrocorn stands proud on four sturdy hooved legs, its woolly brown coat now thick and rough-textured. The single horn has grown into a sweeping curved ram's horn, broad and ridged like a spiral of layered sandstone. Its dark eyes are calm and appraising, and it moves across the amber desert with a confident swagger that makes younger creatures step aside. When charging, it lowers its head so that only the horn hits - it has shattered boulders twice its own size without flinching. The crack of its impact echoes off the amber cliffs and signals to its herd that a new path is clear.

Field note: A Petrocorn can identify the age of any desert boulder by licking it once - a trait no geologist has been able to explain.

About Stonarch

Stonarch is a massive four-legged beast whose grey-brown hide has petrified to the texture of weathered desert stone. Three thick curved horns fan out from its broad head - the central one enormous and cracked at the tip from a lifetime of headbutts, the two flanking ones coiled with deep spiral ridges. Its barrel-round body is covered in natural rock patches that look like natural desert outcroppings. Stonarch moves so rarely that amber desert maps mark its preferred resting spots as landmarks. The guardians say that where Stonarch stands at sunrise, the desert will hold firm against Abalont's creeping dark for another season.

Field note: The three horns of a Stonarch form a natural compass: the tallest always points toward the nearest underground water source.
