About Cubear

Cubear is a wide, dark-blue bear cub with a stout face, round ears, and a row of short spine-like ridges budding along its back. It walks with a slight waddle that it will lose as it grows. In the Sand Dunes it follows the morning shadows cast by great dunes, resting when the shadow is cool and walking when it warms - a natural navigator for a landscape that shifts nightly. When it finds a name it has never heard carved into dune-drift monuments, it sits in front of it and refuses to leave until it feels it has understood.

Field note: Cubear cubs sit in front of any carved inscription they encounter for an average of four minutes before moving on - researchers cannot explain what they are doing.

About Ursolt

Ursolt is a heavy, darkly-coloured bear with well-developed spines along its back and thick, sand-worn paws. Its shoulders are broad and its stride deliberate. It still follows the shadow-paths of the dunes, but now it also makes them - the wide furrow of its passage creates a reliable windbreak other Sand Dunes creatures shelter behind. When it stands upright to survey the horizon, it can see for miles across the flat sea of sand, and it remembers every landmark in three hundred and sixty degrees.

Field note: Ursolt's rear-track furrow is consistent enough that Sand Dune traders use old Ursolt trails as navigational paths - they stay stable for weeks.

About Ursovex

Ursovex is immense - a towering dark bear whose back-spines have grown into a dramatic crown of ridges, whose paws leave deep impressions even in packed desert, and whose presence on a dune ridge is visible from the nearest oasis. It does not roar to announce itself; it simply stands where all can see and that is sufficient. The Sand Dunes' most dangerous crossing routes all pass through Ursovex territory - and all of them are safe because of it. Children taking their first long walk in this region are told: if you lose your way, find Ursovex's ridge and you are found.

Field note: Ursovex has never moved from the same ridge at sunrise in eleven years of observations - it is the most reliable fixed point in the entire Sand Dunes region.
