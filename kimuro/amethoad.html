About Amethoad

Amethoad is a squat green toad with a wide flat body, four stubby legs, and several round purple amethyst bumps clustered along its back like a little gem collection. Its large single eye - a startling magenta - dominates its face. In Amber Desert's night-cool sand hollows it hunkers low, using its gem bumps to absorb heat by day and release it slowly at night. Desert travelers find Amehtoad's bumps glow faintly in the dark - a warm, reassuring lighthouse in the dunes.

Field note: Amethoad's gem bumps glow brightest at the exact temperature where scorpions become sluggish - desert travelers treat this as a safe-sleep signal.

About Gemsaur

Gemsaur is a broad green toad whose back now carries a dense cluster of large amethyst and purple gem formations that jut out like a cactus spine garden. Its wide body is low and powerful, and its single large eye now has a knowing squint. In Amber Desert it half-buries itself in sand with only its gems visible, luring heat-seeking creatures close - then exhales a toxic vapor puff that smells faintly of hot sand and wet stone. Ancient desert merchants used Gemsaur gem-patterns as natural compass markers.

Field note: Gemsaur's gem cluster always points the same direction when it buries itself - desert navigators call this the 'poison star'.

About Amethyrex

Amethyrex has grown into an enormous wide-bodied toad with a full jewel crown of oversized amethyst gems replacing its head-bumps, earning its regal look. Its wide inflated throat-sac glows purple when it calls, and the force of its exhale can push a full grown adult backward three steps. In Amber Desert it sits at the center of oases like an ancient stone idol, and the poison vapors it exhales keep larger predators at a respectful distance. Legend says Amethyrex breathed out a whole valley of clear air when Abalont's shadow first touched the desert.

Field note: When Amethyrex inflates its gem-ringed throat sac fully, the color tells the whole desert the next day's weather - purple for storm, amber for clear.
