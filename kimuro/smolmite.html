About Smolmite

Smolmite is a round orange-and-red caterpillar with plump segmented rings patterned in dark dots, a cheerful rounded face, and tiny stubby leg-nubs it uses to inch along branches with charming determination. A small orange glow pulses at its tail-segment like a warning lantern. It leaves tiny scorch-marks on the leaves it climbs, and the bug thicket guides warn learners not to sit too long on a log Smolmite has recently passed over. It eats fire-seeds it finds among the thicket's charcoal-core roots, growing plumper with each one.

Field note: Smolmite can eat fire-seeds that would torch a glove - it hums contentedly as the seeds warm its belly from the inside.

About Chrysalurn

Chrysalurn is a sealed cocoon of deep amber and bronze, its surface etched with flame-patterns that glow orange at the seams, as though fire is being sculpted into shape within. Flames lick upward from the base of the cocoon in a slow, steady ring. It hangs from the thickest branches of the bug thicket, and the heat it radiates keeps other creatures at a respectful distance. Those who listen closely on quiet nights say they can hear a faint rapid heartbeat inside - or perhaps just the soft roar of something building toward a future it has not yet imagined.

Field note: The temperature of Chrysalurn's outer shell rises by exactly one degree each day of its transformation, peaking the moment it opens.

About Infernaflare

Infernaflare bursts from its cocoon as a blazing moth-like creature with four broad wings of living fire - orange, gold, and deep red, edged in black - that leave trails of bright sparks with every beat. Its body is dark and armored, and its compound eyes burn white-hot. It circles the bug thicket in spiraling updrafts of its own making, and every wingbeat scatters glowing motes that drift down like tiny stars. The thicket creatures treat its arrival as a signal - when Infernaflare circles at dusk, a new lesson is about to be learned.

Field note: Infernaflare's wing-sparks cool into tiny glass beads on the ground - children string them into bracelets for courage.
