About Padsprout

Padsprout is a tiny teal toad with enormous shining blue eyes and a pair of small leaf-wings that flutter from its back when it hops - not quite enough to fly, but enough to make every jump twice as far as its legs alone could manage. It bounces through the lily-padded pools of Verdant Valley, landing on every floating leaf with a small plop and a satisfied blink. It is the first creature many children meet in the Valley, and it greets them with the same generous enthusiasm it gives to everything, including puddles.

Field note: Padsprout's leaf-wings fold flat when rain is coming and open wide when sun is expected - valley farmers rely on them as weather indicators.

About Padoctor

Padoctor is a large, calm toad with a broad flat back on which a collar of green leaves has grown, circling its neck like a living ruff. It sits in the central pools of Verdant Valley with unhurried patience, and smaller creatures come to perch on its back and dry their feet. It produces a mild healing nectar from glands in its feet that seeps into the water around it, keeping the pool's other residents healthy. The guardians kept records of Padoctor's presence going back three hundred years - apparently the same spot, possibly the same toad.

Field note: Padoctor's leaf-collar adds one new leaf each spring - a twenty-leaf collar indicates a very long and quiet life.

About Padbloom

Padbloom is a great broad toad crowned with twin pink lotus blossoms that have rooted into its back and grown with it for so long that it is impossible to say where the toad ends and the flowers begin. It sits in Verdant Valley's deepest central pool with the stillness of something that has decided its purpose is to be exactly where it is. The lotus flowers open at dawn and close at dusk in perfect reliability, and the valley's people use Padbloom's blooms to set their morning routines. When Abalont's shadow reached the Valley, Padbloom did not move - and the shadow did not come that way.

Field note: Padbloom's lotus blooms are never the same shade of pink two days in a row - the full color cycle takes exactly 365 days.
