About Manteel

A tiny pale-blue praying mantis, Manteel holds its triangular head cocked to one side as if permanently curious. Its two translucent wings - barely larger than fallen petals - beat so fast they blur to mist. It perches on tall grass stems in the bug thicket, swaying to feel the breeze before each tiny leap. Guardians who first met Manteel say it would tilt its head in exactly the same way a child does when trying to remember a name it has never heard.

Field note: Manteel's wings beat 200 times per second - the breeze they make is cool enough to chill a fingertip held close.

About Manteelid

Manteelid stands upright on four legs, its two serrated forearms raised at ready like paired blades of sky-blue chitin. Its wings have grown wide and veined, humming in a lower register. It patrols wind channels between the thicket's tall trunks, riding air currents to stay aloft for minutes at a time. When a strong gust arrives, Manteelid spreads all four wings at once and lets out a sharp tone - it seems to sing to the wind like a tuning fork.

Field note: Manteelid's forearms vibrate in strong breezes, producing a faint high tone - thicket travelers say it announces coming storms.

About Celestmantis

Celestmantis moves with the unhurried certainty of a creature that has never needed to hurry. Its four vast wings catch the thicket's highest wind streams, and it glides between canopy openings without a single wingstroke for minutes. Its sky-blue chitin has deepened to steel-teal, and its sweeping forearm blades arc forward like twin crescent moons. Old bug-thicket legends say Celestmantis carved the word for 'patience' into the oldest tree with a single blade-stroke.

Field note: Celestmantis can coast on a single breath of wind for eleven minutes without flapping - researchers timed it on a calm morning.
