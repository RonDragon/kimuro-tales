About Scytinch

Scytinch is a soft cream-white larva, its rounded segmented body tapering at both ends, tiny dark eyes peering from the broader end. It lives in the damp leaf litter of Ghost Hollow's floor where mist keeps the ground permanently soft. It feeds on shadow-saturated organic matter - old fallen leaves that the hollow's ghosts have passed through - and with each meal its pale coloring darkens slightly from the inside out. Hollow explorers find them by the faint luminescent sheen their bodies cast on wet leaves at night.

Field note: Scytinch emits a faint pale glow on its underside that's only visible in complete darkness - hollow explorers call it 'ghost-lint.'

About Scytshell

Scytshell has sealed itself in a dark purple chrysalis half-buried in Ghost Hollow's rich soil - only its coiled upper body is visible, the rest anchored deep. Its shell has the dark iridescence of a mussel shell, and ghost-energy flows through it in visible slow pulses. The hollow's wandering spirits are strongly drawn to it, circling the chrysalis for hours at a time. Old hollow-keepers leave offerings of dark seeds near resting Scytshell, believing the chrysalis is protecting something important inside - a memory, a word, a name not yet given.

Field note: Ghost sightings in Ghost Hollow increase by a measurable amount within a ten-metre radius of a resting Scytshell chrysalis.

About Scytarax

Scytarax emerges as a large dark rhinoceros beetle, its shell the deep black of shadow-soaked heartwood and its single massive horn curving upward with a metallic sheen. Wings fold tight against its back but open silently for flight through Ghost Hollow's canopy, where it is nearly invisible against the night sky. It carries all the shadow-memory absorbed during its chrysalis stage, and the ghosts that orbited it during dormancy now follow it at a distance - an escort of light shapes trailing behind a dark form. Guardians believed Scytarax carries the names of the lost, one for each ghost that follows.

Field note: Scytarax has always been seen with exactly as many ghost-lights trailing it as the number of nights it spent in chrysalis - never fewer, never more.
