About Sproutbuck

Sproutbuck is a slim fawn with a warm tan coat and two prominent leaf-tipped ears that catch every forest sound. Two small leaves sprout from the top of its head like a living crown, perking up when it hears something interesting and drooping when it grows bored. It picks its way through the Dark Forest's root-tangles on delicate hooves, always stepping exactly where a previous foot fell, never snapping a twig. When it blinks its wide eyes at a stranger, experienced travelers feel an unexpected urge to speak more quietly.

Field note: Sproutbuck's two head-leaves fold flat against its skull in heavy rain, but extend fully to catch the first drop of post-storm sunlight, acting as a living sundial.

About Leafdoe

Leafdoe stands upright and alert, antlers now branched and tipped with fresh leaf-clusters that nod in any breeze. Its body has slimmed and elongated, and it moves with an upright, almost contemplative posture - more watching than fleeing. In the Dark Forest, where light arrives at strange angles, Leafdoe serves as an informal landmark: travelers note its white belly-patch visible from a distance and orient themselves to it. It never moves away from an approaching child, only turns its head to keep them in sight.

Field note: Leafdoe's antler-leaves are always fresh green, even in midwinter - forest researchers have never found a withered one.

About Verdantarch

Verdantarch is a tall, elegant stag whose antlers branch widely into a full canopy of leaves that shift with every breeze passing through the Dark Forest. Its posture is upright and composed, with a narrow refined face and long legs that carry it silently between the oldest trees. The forest seems to breathe differently when Verdantarch is present - the wind moves in steady, predictable patterns rather than random gusts, as if it is channeling the airflow deliberately. Its antler-canopy has become a shelter for dozens of small forest creatures that nest among its leaf-branches.

Field note: Verdantarch's antler-spread has never been measured exactly the same way twice; it appears to grow new branches after every storm to replace ones lost.
