About Tentakid

Tentakid is a pudgy purple octopus-creature with a short narwhal-style horn on its forehead and four small tentacles that it uses to bounce across the Bug Thicket's wet leaf-litter. The horn glows faintly when Tentakid is curious - which is almost always - and it probes into undergrowth, fallen logs, and any gap it finds with equal optimism. It squirts a faint purple mist when surprised, which smells of overripe berries and disorients anything within half a meter for about a second. In the Bug Thicket, that second counts.

Field note: Tentakid's horn glow intensifies in direct proportion to how interested it is in whatever it is investigating.

About Tentacoil

Tentacoil has grown larger and more confident, its purple body now bearing two upswept curved horns and six thick tentacles that it carries in a half-coiled posture - ready to extend or retract in an instant. It navigates the Bug Thicket's dense undergrowth by reaching tentacles ahead and pulling itself through gaps that seem too narrow for its body. Its poison has become more controlled; rather than misting, it now delivers a precise dose by touch. Insects of the Bug Thicket give Tentacoil a wide berth, and they are smarter about these things than most.

Field note: Tentacoil can thread its body through a gap no wider than its own head by compressing its mantle to a fraction of its resting width.

About Tentamorph

Tentamorph has crowned itself in a bristling ring of purple psychic spines that radiate outward like a crown of dark crystals, and its tentacles are now long, thick, and powerful enough to reshape the Bug Thicket's undergrowth simply by moving through it. Its purple body pulses with stored psychic energy that it radiates passively - creatures within five meters experience mild disorientation that worsens with proximity. The Thicket's oldest trees lean slightly away from the spots where Tentamorph rests. Even the bugs agree it is their overlord.

Field note: The radius of Tentamorph's passive psychic disorientation field has been measured precisely at 4.8 meters under calm conditions.
