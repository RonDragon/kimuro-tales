About Rootlet

Rootlet is a small wild-boar-like creature whose entire hide is textured like rough bark and patchy moss, making it nearly invisible when it curls up against a tree trunk. Two small white tusks curve upward from its snout. It trots through the edges of the Amber Desert where scrub grass meets sand, rooting up buried seeds with its nose and caching them in small earthen pots it digs out with its forelegs. Guardians who visit the desert's green fringes often find a neatly organized grid of soil patches - all Rootlet's work, every one of them planted with something that will grow.

Field note: Rootlet plants seeds in perfect grids and checks on each one daily - the only creature in the desert with a gardening schedule.

About Tuskhide

Tuskhide has grown broad and powerful, its bark-hide deepening and its tusks curving outward in a wide arc. A mane of thick leafy growth sprouts from its shoulders like a green cape. It pushes through the Amber Desert with its head low, using its tusks as plows to turn dry sand over into softer loam, creating planting rows for the scrub plants that follow in its wake. After a year of Tuskhide working a sand-patch, the desert recedes. The region's farmers leave offerings at the edge of the dunes because wherever Tuskhide goes, green follows within a season.

Field note: The narrow green strips crossing the Amber Desert are called Tuskhide roads - each one marks a single year of one creature's walking route.

About Thornusk

Thornusk is a massive, ancient-looking boar whose body has become almost indistinguishable from a living tree stump - bark-plated, covered in moss and thick root-like growth, with enormous tusks thickened at the base with woody knots. A dense forest of living branches and thick grass erupts from its back like a mobile grove. When it stands still in the Amber Desert, smaller animals take shelter beneath its back-growth, and birds nest in the branches. Abalont tried to salt the desert beyond recovery once; Thornusk walked through the salted zone for seven days and left green behind it every step.

Field note: Birds nest in Thornusk's back-growth every spring - there are currently four occupied nests and Thornusk has adjusted its sleep schedule to avoid disturbing them.
