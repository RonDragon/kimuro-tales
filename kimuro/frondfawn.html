About Frondfawn

Frondfawn is a compact green-furred pup with large leaf-shaped ears and a bushy tail that ends in a cluster of round leaves. A yellow sun-disc marking glows on its flank, and the leaf-tufts on its head bounce with every step. It dashes through the Bug Thicket's low undergrowth in excited figure-eights, nose to the ground, following trails of pollen and insect-scent for no reason other than curiosity. Its tiny paws leave leaf-shaped prints in soft soil, as though the forest is stamping itself wherever it goes.

Field note: Frondfawn digs a small hole at the base of every new tree it meets and deposits one seed inside; it has no memory of doing this.

About Frondeen

Frondeen has grown into a powerful four-legged creature with a broad chest and a back covered in overlapping leaf-plates that make a soft clatter when it moves at speed. Its legs are strong and its bearing is confident; it marks the Bug Thicket's territories by pressing its leafy flanks against border-stones as it patrols. A yellow sun-mark still glows on its side, brighter now, and when it stands in a sunbeam the whole clearing seems to warm slightly. Thicket guides call Frondeen the Green Sentry because it always faces the direction from which something new will arrive.

Field note: Frondeen can detect an insect hive hidden inside a hollow tree from twenty meters away and navigate directly to it without circling.

About Frondstag

Frondstag is a great shaggy beast covered head to tail in thick layers of curling leaf-armor, its back rising in a dramatic mane of spiral green fronds and its broad face framed by long flowing leaf-tendrils. Swirling green-and-gold patterns cover every visible surface of its body. It moves heavily but with absolute sureness through the Bug Thicket, and the breeze it generates just by walking rustles the canopy overhead as it passes. Insects that would otherwise swarm in the thicket move aside in an orderly column when Frondstag approaches, as though following an ancient agreement.

Field note: Frondstag's spiral mane-fronds slowly rotate counterclockwise on calm days and clockwise before a storm; the direction has never been wrong.
