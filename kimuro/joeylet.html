About Joeylet

Joeylet in its first stage is a short, earnest brown joey standing upright with oversized round boxing gloves of bright orange that it holds in front of its chest at all times. Its big round eyes are warm and determined, and its long flat feet plant firmly whenever it squares up. It bounces in tight circles when nervous and swings its padded gloves at the air to psych itself up before a challenge. In Verdant Valley it hops between the song-trees practicing rhythm by punching patterns into the bark with satisfying thwacks. Every correct answer gets a little victory bounce.

Field note: Kangaroo's boxing gloves are permanently attached - trainers have searched for years and cannot find a single lace or buckle.

About Kangarock

In its second stage Joeylet has grown taller and leaner, its sandy-brown coat now sleek along its shoulders and arms. The boxing gloves are gone - it carries its powerful forepaws at ready, balanced on its thick haunches and long tail. It moves in bounding leaps that carry it six meters at a time, and the wind of its passing stirs the tall grass of Verdant Valley into spiraling waves. It loves to race the valley winds along the ridge-paths, and local children track its jump-distance record with notched stakes. Its current best is eleven meters, achieved on a Thursday morning.

Field note: Kangaroo's jump-record post is a notched branch near the valley's east ridge - there are forty-seven notches and counting.

About Roostomper

In its final stage Joeylet is massively built - wide across the shoulders, with arms like bundled rope and a scarred, battle-marked chest it carries with quiet pride. Its thick haunches could drive it through a stone wall, and its tail sweeps the ground with deliberate weight. It stands in the valley as its undisputed defender, watching the paths with calm eyes that miss nothing. When it fights for its learners it does not rage - it measures, waits, and delivers one perfectly placed strike that ends the matter. The guardians say its most devastating blow is a whispered word at the right moment.

Field note: Kangaroo never throws the same punch twice in the same bout - it has catalogued over two hundred distinct combinations, each named after a place it has been.
