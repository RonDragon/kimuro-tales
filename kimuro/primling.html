About Primling

Primling is a compact little brown monkey with a tuft of fur that sticks straight up on its head and big round eyes full of mischief. It sits on its haunches and picks at the bug thicket's bark and leaf litter, finding hidden beetles and carrying them around for no clear reason. Its small hands are surprisingly dexterous and it uses them constantly - braiding grass, piling pebbles, sketching shapes in the dirt. Primling is drawn to old carvings on thicket trees and traces them with a careful finger before scampering away, as if storing the shape somewhere private.

Field note: Primling traces every old carving it finds with one finger before moving on - and never traces the same carving twice.

About Simarch

Simarch has grown into a lean, knuckle-walking ape with an angular face and permanent scowl. Its brown fur is wild and unkempt, and it moves through the bug thicket with aggressive confidence, knocking aside roots and branches with its forearms. It communicates by hitting things rhythmically - two fast knocks means danger, three slow knocks means food. When challenged, it stands fully upright and beats its chest, which produces a surprisingly deep booming sound from such a lean frame. Bug thicket travelers who hear that sound know to move sideways, not back, because Simarch always charges straight.

Field note: Simarch beats its chest exactly seven times before any serious charge - never six, never eight.

About Junglord

Junglord is an enormous shaggy primate wearing a heavy mantle of woven leaves and jungle vines draped across its broad shoulders like a living cloak. Its wide flat face is framed by a massive pale mane, and its knuckles are calloused into near-stone hardness. It sits at the center of the bug thicket and lets the forest reorganize itself around its position - vines thicken where it rests, roots rise to form a natural throne. It does not seek out battles but wins them instantly. The thicket's bug-creatures bring it tribute: the most interesting old carvings from the deepest trees, which Junglord studies one by one and never forgets.

Field note: Junglord's vine-mantle grows exactly one new vine for every new friend it makes - the oldest vines at its collar are over forty years long.
