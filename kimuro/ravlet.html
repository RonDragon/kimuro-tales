About Ravlet

Ravlet at its youngest is a compact, dark-feathered raven with deep violet-black plumage and bold yellow feet that grip branches with surprising strength. It has a sharp beak and a bright, watchful eye that misses nothing. In Ghost Hollow it hops between the pale root-knuckles of dead trees, tugging at fungus and dried shadow-moss to build the neatest nest any creature in the region has ever made. The guardians say a Ravlet nest is a better map of the hollow than any they have drawn - the materials are always sourced from the quietest, safest spots.

Field note: A Ravlet nest is built from materials collected in a perfect circle around a safe resting spot - the nest itself marks the safest point in its territory.

About Ravnox

Ravnox has grown into a sleek, intelligent raven with long trailing feathers at the tail and shaggy throat-hackles that fan out when it is interested in something. Its dark purple plumage has an iridescent sheen in moonlight, and its yellow feet grip any surface - even glass-smooth shadow-stone - with ease. It has developed a habit of collecting voices: not objects, but the sound of them, which it stores and replays in imitation. Travelers who camp in Ghost Hollow often wake to their own conversation from the night before echoing from a branch overhead, edited by Ravnox into a single line that somehow makes more sense.

Field note: Ravnox edits overheard conversations down to one line and plays it back at dawn - always the line that mattered.

About Malcorvus

Malcorvus is a vast raven with a wingspan that casts a genuine shadow over the hollow at midday. Its dark wings are edged in deeper purple and streaked with shadow-dark bands, and its yellow eyes glow like two small moons. It moves with the slow gravity of something that has lived through many seasons of Abalont's darkness. In Ghost Hollow it is the keeper of the deepest roots - the names of things that existed before Abalont came. It has never been known to repeat a word frivolously, and when it finally speaks one to a child, that child never forgets it.

Field note: Malcorvus has been heard to use only one hundred and eight distinct words in living memory - every single one is permanently learned by anyone who hears it.
