About Coralite

Coralite is a tiny sea turtle hatchling with a shell patterned in soft teal swirls, four flipper-legs and enormous curious eyes. Its shell markings spiral outward from the center in smooth concentric rings, as if the whole sea is written on its back in one long word. In Coral Bay's shallows it rides the surface current with barely any effort, spinning slowly as the swirl-pattern catches light. When it finds a singing current it spins faster, stirring the water into a bright ring around itself.

Field note: Coralite spins visibly faster the moment it enters a singing current - bay guides use it to find the strongest tides.

About Coraleon

Coraleon has grown branching teal antlers from the front edge of its shell - real living coral-antlers that sway with ocean current and catch drifting song-seeds from the bay. Its flippers have grown broader and it moves with powerful strokes. At midtide it rises to float on its back, antlers breaking the surface, letting birds perch on the tines and carry song-seeds to the shore. The guardians of Coral Bay called this the Teaching Float - it still performs it every third day without prompting.

Field note: Coraleon floats on its back every third day at midtide, holding its coral-antlers above the surface for exactly one hour.

About Coralarch

Coralarch carries a full living reef upon its back - branching coral-trees in orange and gold draped over a massive golden-green shell, ringed in seaflower garlands that never die. Its four great flippers move with the slow power of tides, and when it surfaces to breathe its exhale creates a warm fog that settles over the bay and triggers blooming in every seagrass bed nearby. Every memory-creature in Coral Bay swam through Coralarch's wake at least once in its life.

Field note: A bloom of new seagrass follows Coralarch's path within 24 hours of its passage - bay scholars chart its routes by tracking the growth.
