About Bublet

Bublet is an icy-blue pufferfish with a permanently disgruntled expression that it earned the day it was born and has maintained ever since. Its smooth round body is lightly textured like dimpled sea-glass and its small mouth is set in a downward curve of complete skepticism about everything. Bublet inhabits the cold-current channels of Frozen Peaks, where the icy upwellings keep the water clear and brisk. It has a habit of puffing up slightly - not full, just enough to communicate displeasure - whenever anything passes too close to the particular patch of current it has decided is its.

Field note: Bublet's territory consists of exactly one cold-current patch two meters wide - it has never expanded it or abandoned it.

About Buboink

Buboink has grown a proper set of short, sharp spines across its body that it deploys in a ring when threatened, each one carrying a thin coating of ice that freezes anything pressing against it. Its expression has not improved with size - if anything, the larger face provides more surface area for displeasure. Buboink guards the cold-channel entrances of Frozen Peaks with the humorless efficiency of a toll collector who has never once been surprised to find someone trying to sneak past. It inflates to full size at the first sign of any trespass and holds it until the trespasser has been gone for at least five minutes.

Field note: Buboink remains fully inflated for exactly five minutes after a trespasser leaves its territory - it has been measured repeatedly.

About Behemoth

Behemoth is an enormous ancient pufferfish whose spines have grown into long crystalline ice-lances covering every surface, and whose fully-inflated form is wide enough to plug a glacial outflow entirely. It is deeply grumpy in the way that only very old things that have watched everything go wrong for a very long time can be. Behemoth holds station in the deepest meltwater trench under Frozen Peaks, and the whole range's temperature stays stable as long as it rests there. The peaks-folk say the day Behemoth leaves its trench the thaw begins - and they are probably right.

Field note: Behemoth has occupied the same meltwater trench for so long that the lakebed has permanently depressed around it by two meters.
