About Quilkin

Quilkin is a small green-brown pangolin-lizard whose overlapping scales have a mossy texture that blends perfectly with the crystal mine walls. Its large purple eyes stand out vividly against the earthy tones, giving it a look of perpetual alertness. It moves slowly, scale by scale, feeling the mine walls for vibration before committing to each step. The crystal mines' deep tunnels are its home, and it memorizes every crack and crystal cluster in its territory - explorers say if you follow a Quilkin rather than your map, you will never get lost in the mines.

Field note: Quilkin's scales are slightly magnetic; small crystal fragments cling to its sides and it collects them deliberately, then drops them in specific spots.

About Quillvern

Quillvern has grown longer and heavier with enlarged crystal-studded scale armor that catches and refracts the mine lanterns into moving rainbow patches on the tunnel walls. Its purple eyes are keener now, able to see mineral veins in rock that experienced miners miss. When threatened it rolls into a tight scale-ball that no mine tool can dent and waits, perfectly patient, until the threat passes - which has taken as long as four days by some accounts. The crystal mines' deepest-level workers consider Quillvern a colleague rather than a creature, and it seems to agree.

Field note: Quillvern taps the mine wall exactly three times before turning a corner - a habit the miners adopted and now use universally as a safe-passage signal.

About Quilarch

Quilarch is vast and low-slung - a pangolin the size of a small cart, armored in enormous overlapping crystal-rock plates that fracture light into prismatic fans across the mine ceilings. Its purple eyes glow with a faint inner light in the deepest tunnels where no lantern reaches. It walks the oldest mine passages at its own slow pace, and when it rolls into a ball every crystal in the surrounding area rings softly. The guardians believe Quilarch is the living memory of the crystal mines - that each scale it has grown carries the mineral signature of the exact tunnel it was formed in, mapping its entire life as a geological record.

Field note: Quilarch's eldest scales are as hard as the mine's own crystal - researchers have been unable to mark them with any tool available in the region.
