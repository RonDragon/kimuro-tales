About Glacen

Glacen is a chubby segmented larva made of translucent pale-blue ice-like rings, each segment glowing faintly from within. Two tiny antennae wobble on its head and its wide round eyes give it an endlessly curious expression. It rolls rather than crawls along the tidal rocks of Frozen Peaks, collecting minerals from the spray. At night the glow between its segments pulses in a slow rhythm, and local fishers use it as a lantern to find their moorings in the dark bay.

Field note: Press a Glacen gently and all its segments chime together with a faint crystalline tinkle.

About Crystend

In its middle stage Crystend appears as a large faceted blue-and-white crystal - diamond-shaped, floating just above the frost-packed ground in a subtle shimmer. It has shed the larval body entirely and retreated into a pure crystalline form, its internal light refracting through every facet into tiny rainbows across the snow. Creatures approach the glittering stone expecting treasure, but it pulses warmth when touched, startling them away. The guardians of Frozen Peaks believe it is holding something inside the light, waiting for someone patient enough to sit with it.

Field note: Each facet of Crystend reflects a different colour, and studying which one shines tells attentive trainers the time of day.

About Prismafly

Prismafly emerges from the crystal with vast iridescent wings - pale lavender and sky-blue - that scatter light into full prismatic fans when they beat. Its small compact body is vivid blue-purple, crowned with two curling antennae, and it floats above the ice shelf in slow graceful arcs. Every wingbeat sends a visible ripple of coloured light washing across the ice. The creatures of Frozen Peaks gather beneath it during the noon sun, basking in its rainbow canopy, and it has never once landed since its first flight.

Field note: Prismafly's wings beat exactly 4,200 times per minute - researchers verify this is also the frequency of the glacier's daily creep.
