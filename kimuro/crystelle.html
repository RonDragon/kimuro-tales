About Crystelle

Crystelle spends its early life as a closed oyster shell resting at the bottom of Poison Marsh's quieter pools, its two grey-white shells sealed tight around a glowing blue crystal gem it has been growing since before it was born. A ring of faint blue light seeps from the shell's seam when the gem inside pulses. It is perfectly still for days at a time, filtering toxins from the marsh water through its steel-hard shells and locking them away inside its crystalline core. Marsh ecologists believe Crystelle seedlings are responsible for the marsh's clean-water pockets appearing exactly where they are most needed.

Field note: The blue gem growing inside each Crystelle contains no toxins despite filtering marsh water constantly - it converts them entirely into crystal mass.

About Shellume

Shellume has cracked open and emerged as a multi-legged crustacean with a pale armored body, six jointed legs tipped with steel-hard points, and a rough domed shell that has absorbed enough metal from the marsh minerals to ring like a bell when struck. A single large blue eye sits above its wide mouth. It walks upside-down on the ceiling of underwater rock formations for days, filtering the water overhead and occasionally dropping crystals that accumulate into formations on the marsh floor below. Researchers mapping the marsh use Shellume crystal deposits as waypoints.

Field note: Shellume's blue eye can see through murky water as clearly as clean air - marsh explorers claim following one through a fog bank leads to the shortest route every single time.

About Geomaxon

Geomaxon is massive and ancient, a hulking armored crab-golem whose body segments are thick steel-hard shell plates worn smooth by centuries in the marsh. A single enormous eye glows deep blue from its broad face, and its wide body is ringed with gold-hued detail lines like welded seams on a forged vessel. Its eight legs are powerful enough to walk on the marsh floor during high water, displacing everything nearby with its slow movement. Geomaxon anchors the Poison Marsh's deepest channels - its body is old enough that the entire marsh has reshaped around where it chooses to rest.

Field note: The gold ring markings on Geomaxon's shell are the only part that is not natural - they were etched by an unknown hand in an age before recorded history, and they have never faded.
