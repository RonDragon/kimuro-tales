About Thornymph

A spiky, electric-green mantis nymph no bigger than a hand, Thornymph clings to stems in the Bug Thicket with three pairs of barbed legs and turns its oversized eyes toward any movement with methodical precision. Its entire body is studded with small defensive spike-protrusions, and yellow accent dots along its back flash in mimicry of flower pollen to confuse small predators. It chirps to other Thornymphs by rubbing its forelegs together, creating a high clicking rhythm that thicket children recognize as a bug-choir. It eats only the tiniest bugs, and apologizes to each one by waving an antenna.

Field note: Thornymph mimics flower-pollen markings so well that actual pollinators try to land on it - it waits patiently until they give up.

About Spiculid

Spiculid stands nearly as tall as a child's shoulder, its lean green body now hardened into segmented chitinous armour and its forelimbs extending into long hooked blade-scythes. A serrated ridge runs down its back like a saw, and its multifaceted eyes track movement in a full 300-degree arc. It stakes out a meter-wide hunting territory in the Bug Thicket that it marks by slashing leaf-stems with surgical precision. Spiculid never charges directly - it circles, waits, and strikes from exactly the angle its prey cannot see.

Field note: Spiculid ambush-positions are always on the prey's blind side; every recorded strike comes from outside the target's 60-degree rear arc.

About Phalanx

Phalanx is the undisputed apex of the Bug Thicket - a great winged mantis warrior standing head-high to a guardian, with a full spread of veined leaf-wings that open with a sharp crack when it decides to assert itself. Its blade-forelegs are now long enough to cross over its chest like armoured greatswords. When Phalanx spreads its wings and stridulates - rubbing wing-to-body at full vibration - the sound carries through the entire thicket and every smaller creature goes still. The guardians say Phalanx has commanded every insect battle in the thicket since before the first nest was built there.

Field note: When Phalanx opens its wings fully, measured vibration from the stridulation has knocked leaves from trees thirty metres away.
