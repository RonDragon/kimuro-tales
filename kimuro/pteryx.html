About Pteryx

Pteryx is a small tan-gold bat creature with a round compact body, wide leathery wings folded against its sides, and enormous round eyes that catch every flicker of lava-light. Two small pointed ears sit at the top of its round head, and its toothy little grin is both unsettling and charming. It lives in the lava crater's high cliff-niches, hanging upside-down in groups and chattering. When startled, it launches into the air with a surprised squeak and circles back immediately to reinvestigate the source of the fright. Lava crater climbers say Pteryx will follow a warm lantern for hours, mistaking it for a particularly interesting volcanic vent.

Field note: Pteryx always lands back on the same exact rock niche it launched from, within two centimeters, no matter how far it flew.

About Pterygon

Pterygon has grown into a lean, two-legged wyvern with long membranous wings, a long neck, and a narrow flat-skulled head full of sharp teeth. Its gold-tan scales have a subtle metallic sheen, and its hind legs are strong enough to launch it vertically from a standing start. It glides the lava crater's thermal columns for hours without landing, banking on outstretched wings with confident elegance. Its dark eyes see equally well in full sun and crater-dark. Pterygon is possessive of its thermal columns and will chase any other flying creature out of its territory with sharp snapping dives, pulling away at the last possible moment every time.

Field note: Pterygon memorizes the shape of every thermal column in its territory and adjusts its glide by a single degree whenever one changes.

About Drakopteryx

Drakopteryx is a full dragon of the lava crater - broad-shouldered, four-limbed with massive leathery wings, and a head crowned with swept-back crests and fang-flanked jaws. Its gold-brass scales are heavy and layered, and the darker patches on its flanks and wing-membranes absorb volcanic heat and radiate it back as shimmering waves. When it beats its wings on takeoff, loose rock pellets are thrown hundreds of meters. The lava crater's oldest record describes Drakopteryx as the dragon that sealed Abalont's first passage through the crater by breathing a column of superheated air into the vent and collapsing it. The vent is still sealed. The record notes it never needed to do it again.

Field note: Drakopteryx has sealed every unstable volcanic vent in its crater territory with a single breath - geological surveys confirm none have reopened.
