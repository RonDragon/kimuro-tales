About Frostcalf

A small, round-bodied calf coated in pale blue-white fur, Frostcalf has a single short ice-blue horn jutting from its forehead and soft swirl markings across its flank. Its stubby legs barely keep its belly off the ground. Found near the frozen edges of the Poison Marsh where cold mist pools at dawn, it licks mineral-rich ice patches and leaves tiny hoof-prints that never fully melt. The guardians once carved its image into the first frost-stones as a symbol of endurance.

Field note: Frostcalf's single horn glows faint blue just before temperature drops sharply in the marsh.

About Tundhorn

Tundhorn stands upright on four sturdy legs, its broad body covered in shaggy pale-blue fur. Two curved horns of solid ice now crown its head, and its wide nostrils breathe out plumes of frozen mist. The swirl markings have darkened into icy ridges along its back. It patrols the outer marshlands, pressing its horns into the boggy soil to freeze narrow paths for smaller creatures to cross safely. Trainers say it always knows where firm ground lies even in the darkest fog.

Field note: One headbutt from Tundhorn freezes a full metre of bogwater solid in under two seconds.

About Boralord

Massive and glacier-slow, Boralord is encased from shoulder to hoof in jagged blue ice armour that has grown over its body through years in the cold marsh. Its sweeping curved horns have thickened into enormous blue-white slabs wreathed in perpetual frost vapour, and its eyes glow like frozen amber beneath a heavy ice-brow. When it charges, the ground underneath crystallises in its wake, leaving a glassy trail that lasts for days. Ancient marsh-dwellers call it the Cold Warden - saying it freezes the most toxic bog-pools each winter so they cannot spread.

Field note: The ice armour on a Boralord's back has been accumulating for decades; no two patterns are ever the same.
