About Gemurp

Gemurp is an endearing round brown bear cub with warm amber fur and a cluster of small teal-green gem crystals sprouting from between its shoulder blades like a natural backpack it forgot was there. Its large round paws dig happily into the rocky soil of Granite Heights, unearthing mineral deposits it licks with great concentration. Though it looks soft, the gem cluster on its back is harder than granite, and nuzzling against it accidentally is genuinely uncomfortable. Gemurp collects shiny pebbles and arranges them in patterns around its sleeping spot each night.

Field note: Every crystal cluster Gemurp deposits around its sleeping spot has been verified as a different mineral type - it never places the same mineral twice in a row.

About Gemurite

Gemurite has grown into a powerful adult bear with broad shoulders, and the teal-green crystal cluster on its back has expanded into a full ridge of sharp gem spikes running from its neck to its tail base. It still has the warm brown fur of its youth, but it is now shot through with mineral deposits that make its coat feel like touching fine-grain sandstone. Gemurite stamps out territories in Granite Heights by scoring the rock faces with its crystal ridge, leaving vivid green lines that serve as road markers for other creatures. Miners follow those marks to find the richest veins.

Field note: The teal crystal spikes on Gemurite's back have been verified as a mineral not found anywhere else in the world - only on Gemurites.

About Crystallion

Crystallion is enormous and magnificent, its entire dorsal surface now encased in dense overlapping teal-green crystal armor that has fused with its spine and ribs into a permanent geological formation. When it walks, the crystal plates grind and shift with a sound like a small avalanche. Its massive crystal claws can cleave solid granite with three swipes. Crystallion's territory encompasses the highest peaks of Granite Heights, where it sleeps for months at a time in deep winter - during which time, lichens and smaller crystals grow over the exposed portions of its crystal ridge, and the mountains seem to breathe more slowly.

Field note: When Crystallion wakes from its winter sleep, it shakes, and the sound of the crystal avalanche from its back can be heard in the valley below - residents take it as a sign to begin spring planting.
