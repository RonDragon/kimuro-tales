About Pinqulet

Pinqulet is a tiny pink-bodied sea creature that curves and bounces through Coral Bay's shallows like a living toy. Its round belly and upturned snout are a soft bubblegum pink, and its cheerful blue eyes are huge and round. A small dorsal fin and a pair of side fins are all it needs to zip around the coral gardens. It has already discovered that staring at the coral polyps causes them to briefly glow pink - an accidental psychic pulse it triggers whenever it blinks at a new creature. Young reef-tenders keep Pinqulets as companions because they alert the bay when storm-waters are coming by spinning in circles.

Field note: Pinqulet spins left-clockwise when rain is an hour away, and right-clockwise when it's ten minutes away.

About Pinquale

Pinquale has grown into a sleek, confident swimmer - its body still blush pink but now streamlined, with a longer snout and a confident tail fluke. The side fins have broadened to help it cruise at speed through the bay's deeper channels. Its wide eyes see through murky coral-bay waters as clearly as glass, a psychic sharpening that lets it notice things other creatures miss entirely. It leaps from the water in low arcs at dawn, the spray catching the morning light and scattering it in brief rainbows that the bay's guardian markers track. Pinquale is said to remember every voice raised in its presence, even those whispered under the water.

Field note: Pinquale's wake always curves in the shape of the last word it heard spoken above the water's surface.

About Pinquarch

Pinquarch is a magnificent full-grown sea creature whose pink body is now adorned with a glowing golden halo that hovers above its head. Pink feather-like fins trail from its back and flanks, shimmering with iridescent patterns as it glides. Its golden tail-fluke sweeps water into gentle spirals that glow faintly where they touch the coral. The bay's oldest records describe Pinquarch as a living tide-reader - it knows when Abalont's shadow touches the water before any storm appears, and its halo dims as warning. Children who touch its flank claim they briefly understand every language spoken by the sea.

Field note: When Pinquarch's golden halo turns copper-orange at sunset, the bay clams all close their shells simultaneously.
