About Anchorpup

Anchorpup is a stocky dark-grey crab with a rounded shell etched with anchor symbols and bright orange hair-like frills sprouting from its head and claws like a rocky sea-creature's crown. Two small anchor-engraved shields sit embedded in its shell. It scuttles sideways along the granite coastlines where Granite Heights meets the sea, wedging itself into rock crevices when the tide comes in. Young Anchorpups anchor themselves so firmly that fishermen have been known to mistake them for actual fixed harbor hardware.

Field note: Anchorpup can lock its legs into a rock crack so tightly that twenty children pulling together cannot move it.

About Anchoreel

Anchoreel has grown into an imposing dark slate-blue crab with a massive ridged shell and three large decorative anchor-shields embedded across its back and claws. Its orange crest-frills have thickened into dense tufts around its eyes and flanks, giving it a wild salt-spray look. Its claws can grip ocean boulders bare enough to strip barnacles in one pull. In Granite Heights' tidal pools it acts as an anchor for smaller creatures during storms - they cling to its shell as it braces against currents that would sweep them away.

Field note: In a gale, up to forty smaller creatures have been counted sheltering on a single Anchoreel's shell - it never seems to notice the weight.

About Colossamchor

Colossamchor is a fortress of a crab - massive dark stone body with a shell as thick as harbor walls, orange coral-frills cascading from its head and flanks, and enormous anchor emblems carved deep into its two front shields. Its claws could redirect a river. It stands at the granite cliffs where the great ocean meets the Heights, and the tide literally rises and falls a few centimeters differently when it shifts position. Ancient mariners built their first harbor walls by tracing the curve of a sleeping Colossamchor.

Field note: The tidal rhythm at Granite Heights Cove has matched Colossamchor's breathing pattern for three hundred recorded years.
