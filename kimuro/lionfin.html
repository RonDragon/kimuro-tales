About Lionfin

Lionfin is a vivid little lionfish - brilliant red and white vertical stripes across a compact body, with a fan of long venomous spines spreading above and around it like a tiny crown. Its large purple eye watches everything with bright suspicion. It drifts near coral bay's shallower reef heads, barely moving unless something comes too close, at which point the spine fan snaps wide. Reef fish twice its size give it a lane of clear water. The guardians say Lionfin's colour warns: 'I have something to teach, and it may sting.'

Field note: Lionfin can hold perfectly still for over an hour and is frequently mistaken for a piece of stinging coral by passing fish.

About Spinfin

Spinfin has grown into a bold, full-bodied lionfish with an extravagant fan of white-edged pectoral fins spreading wide on both sides and a full dorsal spine array. Its red-and-white stripes deepen toward crimson, and its large eye holds a steady purple glow. It moves through coral bay mid-water with slow wing-like fin sweeps, ruling a patch of reef with complete confidence. Smaller creatures never challenge it directly - they simply reroute. In the bay's old tales it represents a word once forgotten that returns with twice the force.

Field note: Spinfin's venom changes flavour with the season - bitter in winter, sharp in summer - and reef researchers taste-test it with extreme caution.

About Spinaryx

Spinaryx wears a crown of long ornate spines that trail like royal ribbons, its body now a deep crimson-violet with purple-tipped fan fins that spread wider than a child's arm-span on each side. A faint toxic shimmer surrounds it in the water. It moves rarely and only with purpose, and when it does the entire reef falls still. In coral bay legend, Spinaryx holds the memories of every creature that ever learned something and passed it on - each spine tip carries a word the reef will not let be forgotten.

Field note: The water within an arm's length of Spinaryx glows faintly purple at night - enough to read by if you hold a page very still.
