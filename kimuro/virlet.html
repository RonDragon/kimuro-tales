About Virlet

Virlet is a small, coiled green serpent wound tight as a spring, its scales textured like woven vine-cord and its round yellow eyes wide with alert curiosity. Even at this young age, leaf-like frills edge its back and tiny yellow flower-buds dot its coils. It lives among the cooled lava-flow channels of the crater's rim where hardy grass and creepers push between rocks - a bold little creature threading through the volcanic debris. Virlet wraps itself around anything new it finds, investigating with its whole body rather than just its eyes.

Field note: Virlet's coils are strong enough to crack a green-wood branch; it wraps saplings at night and releases them at dawn with a slight bow-shape pressed in.

About Viradon

Viradon twists its vine-armored body into elegant figure-of-eight loops, its scales now deeply knotted like intertwined rope-vines and decorated with leaf-fronds in full green. Its yellow eyes have sharpened with dragon intelligence, and it can uncoil with explosive speed when it chooses. It navigates the lava crater's vegetation-choked side channels with ease, using its body as both anchor and tool - bracing against one rock face to lever another aside. The crater's edge-scrub has grown thicker over the years wherever Viradon has nested, as if nurtured by its presence.

Field note: Viradon uses its strongest coil-loop as a sling, launching smooth rocks with precision at distances of up to fifteen metres.

About Viranthus

Viranthus fills the crater floor with its vast interlocking coils, body thick as a storm-felled tree and wrapped so densely in overlapping vine-armor that it resembles a sleeping forest more than a creature. Its broad flat head rests above the loops, watching the crater rim with ancient patience. Green blossoms erupt from its scales each spring regardless of the volcanic heat below. The crater's oldest legend says Viranthus arrived when the lava first cooled and planted the first seeds - that every green thing in this region of the world grew from its body-heat and breath. Its roar shakes ash loose from the crater walls.

Field note: Viranthus's scales are so intergrown with living vine that botanists cannot tell where the creature ends and the plant begins on its lower coils.
