About Azuspark

Azuspark dashes along the Steel Fortress's high parapet walls, its cobalt-blue fur trimmed in gold catching every crosswind and generating sparks where the current meets its skin. The orange tail flicks upward the moment a wind shift occurs - a reliable early-warning signal for the fortress's flag-crews. Azuspark is small enough to sprint through the maintenance tunnels beneath the walls, carrying messages faster than any signal drum. It snatches words from the air with visible delight, pausing mid-sprint to repeat a new one loudly before racing onward.

Field note: Azuspark's tail lifts exactly three seconds before every wind shift - the fortress flag-crew never needs a weather-vane while it is on duty.

About Azurek

Azurek lopes the fortress ramparts with its thick ruffled gold-and-blue mane streaming behind it like a battle pennant. Every paw-strike on the stone produces a small crackle of static; on gusty nights its whole outline glows with a pale blue-gold corona. The fortress records show Azurek once ran the full perimeter - all three kilometers - in under two minutes during a crisis, its speed creating a visible shock-wave that stripped the banners clean from their poles. Soldiers leave scratch-behind-the-ear tributes on the west tower for good patrols.

Field note: A single lap of the fortress by Azurek at full sprint creates a shock-wave still detectable on instruments in the inner courtyard.

About Azuranth

Azuranth stands broad and blazing at the fortress's highest point, its enormous electric mane whipping in the permanent wind that surrounds it. The gold-blue lightning markings on its flanks have deepened to near-white at the center, and its roar combines the crack of lightning with the howl of a gale into a single sound that empties the sky of birds for an hour. The fortress's oldest stones carry claw-marks where Azuranth sharpened its claws; the engineers consider the marks load-bearing.

Field note: Azuranth's claw-marks on the fortress's eastern tower have never eroded in a thousand years - the stone around them is visibly reinforced.
