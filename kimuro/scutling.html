About Scutling

Scutling is a small green isopod - a pill-bug shape with a gently domed back and six stubby legs that carry it in a slow, bobbing walk. It lives in the mossy crevices of Granite Heights' lower ledges where water seeps between rocks, feeding on mineral-rich algae mats. When frightened, it tucks its legs under its body and becomes a smooth pebble-sized lump impossible to distinguish from the rocks around it. The heights' children collect them into small mossy jars, calling them 'living pebbles.'

Field note: When curled, a Scutling holds its position with a grip equivalent to four times its body weight - trying to pry one open takes real effort.

About Scutilion

Scutilion has grown flat and wide, its olive-green body now segmented into overlapping plates that slide over each other as it moves - a sound like shuffling stone tiles announces its presence before it is seen. It navigates Granite Heights' steep faces by gripping with all six legs simultaneously, and it prefers to walk along vertical cliff surfaces rather than the flat paths, hugging the rock with each plate of its body making full contact. Its antenna tips brush every crack as it passes, cataloging the mineral content below.

Field note: Scutilion can travel along a vertical granite face for up to three consecutive hours without rest - it has never been recorded falling.

About Scutirax

Scutirax is a great golden-tan armored titan, its overlapping body plates now thick as quarried stone and worn smooth by years of cliff-face traversal. Many sturdy legs carry its long broad body with slow hydraulic precision, and its bulk is enough to reshape small rock formations just by pressing through them. Granite Heights' stone-paths are partly formed by Scutirax's regular routes, worn down by centuries of its weight. Guardians say it was here before the first path was carved - the original road-builder of the heights.

Field note: A well-worn path across Granite Heights was traced back to a Scutirax route - the creature had used it for an estimated 300 years before humans arrived.
