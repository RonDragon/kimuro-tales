About Scorcule

Scorcule is a small glowing orange worm, its segmented body curled in a comma shape and pulsing with inner heat. It lives in the scorched underside of Sand Dunes rocks where the surface temperature could blister skin - Scorcule absorbs that heat and stores it, making its body warm to the touch like a smooth river stone left in afternoon sun. It has no legs, but ripples through hot sand at surprising speed by contracting its body segments rhythmically. The dunes' midnight travelers sometimes mistake a cluster of them for a bed of embers.

Field note: A single Scorcule emits enough body heat to keep a two-person tent warm through a cold desert night.

About Scorvenom

Scorvenom has grown a long multi-legged body, each segment bordered in deep orange and bearing a pair of sharp legs that leave tiny scorch marks as it walks. Its face is fierce, with mandibles that drip a heated venom that sears rather than poisons - a unique chemical it brews from volcanic mineral dust it ingests off hot dune surfaces. It is the apex ambush hunter of the dune underside: it lies pressed flat against hot rock, indistinguishable from a crack in the stone, until something small crosses its shadow.

Field note: Scorvenom's venom does not injure by toxin but by a rapid spike of heat - a drop on bare skin produces a mark like a tiny brand.

About Infermarx

Infermarx coils like a small inferno - a great fire-patterned centipede whose entire body blazes with interlocking flame-scale markings. Its face has grown dragon-fierce, with wide curved mandibles and eyes like heated coals. A visible heat haze surrounds it at all times, and flames flicker briefly at the tips of each leg as it moves. It has claimed the deepest dune basin of Sand Dunes as its territory, and the scorched spiral trail it leaves when it hunts is visible from the ridge above. Guardians call it the Buried Flame - the fire that refused to go out.

Field note: Infermarx leaves a spiral scorch trail up to 80 metres long when hunting - the pattern repeats identically, as if following an invisible groove.
