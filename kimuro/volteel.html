About Volteel

A slender blue eel no longer than a child's forearm, Volteel bobs upright in warm shallows with two tiny bright antenna-horns quivering on its snout. Its long ribbon tail fans out like a sail to keep it steady in the tidal drift. In Coral Bay's reef gardens, young Volteel hatch in clusters beneath lightning-struck coral formations and immediately begin hunting the tiny sparks that crackle along broken reef edges - learning to call home by following the electric taste of the water.

Field note: Volteel navigates entirely by electric taste - it flicks its horns like antennae to sample the charge in the current.

About Amphydra

Amphydra's scales have darkened to deep blue, and its antenna-horns have lengthened into crackling prongs that fork the water ahead of it. A bright electric aura pulses along the full length of its body, and its large round eyes have turned pitch-black, reading the electromagnetic field of every fish within twenty meters. It no longer hunts along the reef - it hunts by waiting at the reef's edge until prey swims into range, then releases a single short discharge.

Field note: Amphydra's electric pulse is precisely calibrated - enough to stun a reef fish, never strong enough to shatter coral.

About Thunderwyrm

Thunderwyrm fills the deepest channels of Coral Bay with its presence - a colossal eel with a fanged dragon's jaw that gapes wide enough to swallow a dinghy whole. Lightning arcs and froths in a continuous wreath around its whole body, and the sea glows pale blue for a hundred meters in every direction when it passes. Ancient sailors charted Coral Bay's reefs by the light it cast, calling it the Wandering Beacon. It speaks in electric pulses that the reef creatures translate into warnings and welcomes.

Field note: The bioluminescent ring around Coral Bay's deepest trench is not coral - it is the resting glow of a sleeping Thunderwyrm.
