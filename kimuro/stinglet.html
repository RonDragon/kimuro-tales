About Stinglet

Stinglet is a tiny flat-bodied ray no bigger than a hand, sandy-tan with a smooth oval disc and a slender whip-tail trailing behind. Its little eyes sit on top of its disc, looking upward with a gentle, curious expression. It glides just above the seafloor of Coral Bay in lazy figure-eights, stirring up small clouds of fine silt that it then fans away with its wing-fins. The guardians first brought it to the surface to show children that even the smallest creature in the bay has a name - and Stinglet has been the starting point for thousands of unfamiliar names ever since.

Field note: Stinglet settles flat on sand and becomes almost perfectly invisible - a patch of seabed that is watching you.

About Raywing

Raywing has grown to the size of a dinner tray, its sandy-brown disc now fringed with small nodular bumps and its tail longer and more flexible. It skims just beneath the surface of Coral Bay, its wingtip fins breaking the water with the faintest ripple. It has learned to use the tidal currents to ride for hours without a single wingbeat, mapping the bay by the taste of the water alone. The bay's fishing families hang a Raywing scale near the door as a luck charm - the creatures are said to know where the safest sand banks are a full tide ahead of time.

Field note: Raywing tastes the current an hour before a tidal shift and always adjusts course - it has never been stranded on a sandbar.

About Thornmanta

Thornmanta is a broad, powerful manta with a wide golden-tan disc studded with rows of sharp thorn-like spines along its back and flanks. Its long tail ends in a serrated point. It patrols the deepest channels of Coral Bay where the reef meets the open sea, holding the boundary. Its passage stirs the sand into long columns that drift upward like smoke signals - the bay's creatures read these columns to know where it has been. Abalont sent a tide of shadow-water to drown the bay's singing corals once; Thornmanta swept it back with three wingbeats so powerful they rearranged the seafloor.

Field note: The sand columns Thornmanta raises persist for a full day - reef creatures use them as navigation markers when currents shift.
