About Scoryte

Scoryte is a compact blue-purple lizard whose orange frill-fins flare from its neck and shoulders like tiny flame-petals. It scurries across Amber Desert rocks with quick four-legged darts, using its bright frills to signal mood - fanned wide means 'back off,' folded flat means 'curious.' The poison it stores is fire-warm, sitting at the back of its throat, and it can spit a short gout of burning venom if really startled. But mostly it would rather show off its frills to a rival than fight.

Field note: Scoryte's frill-fins flush a deeper orange shade when it's angry - tracking the color change is the safest way to judge its mood from a distance.

About Scoraxe

Scoraxe has grown longer and lower, its orange dorsal spines now running from neck to tail-tip in a single unbroken ridge. Purple-and-gold spots mark its flanks in a pattern unique to each individual, and its fire-venom has concentrated enough to leave scorch-stains where it lands on sand. It claims territories in the mid-desert where rocks absorb heat all day, and it presses its belly to warm stone at sunset to charge up. When two Scoraxe share a rock, their spine-colors shift and sync - a communication no one has fully translated.

Field note: The unique spot pattern on each Scoraxe's flank has been used by desert trackers to identify individuals for over 200 years of journals.

About Infernorax

Infernorax is a great dark purple lizard-beast whose tall purple spines run the full length of its back and rise to impressive heights - each one tipped with a venom sac that ruptures under pressure and burns like a brand. It moves deliberately through the Amber Desert's highest heat zones, and the air around it shimmers with combined heat and chemical vapor. Its spine-tips glow faintly at night, making it visible across the dunes - a warning the desert itself broadcasts to everything else. The guardians called it the Wandering Brand - the desert's own signature mark.

Field note: Infernorax's spine-tips are visible glowing at night from up to one kilometre in clear desert air - they pulse slightly in rhythm with its heartbeat.
