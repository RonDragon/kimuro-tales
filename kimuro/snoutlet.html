About Snoutlet

Tuskton's first form is a bright-eyed, warm-brown boar piglet with two small but sharp incipient tusks and a trotting gait so bouncy it seems to have springs in its feet. Its snout is perpetually dusty from rooting around in the mortar between Steel Fortress flagstones. It has no sense of personal space and headbutts friends, strangers, and structural columns with equal enthusiasm. The garrison considers it a mascot - until it discovers where the food stores are.

Field note: Tuskton piglets have been found in seventeen different supply rooms they had no apparent way of entering - no one knows how.

About Tuskhog

Tuskhog is a scarred, shaggy-maned boar with two thick curved tusks, a bristling ridge of dark fur along its spine, and the kind of expression that suggests it has not been surprised by anything in years. Its hooves ring on cobblestones. It patrols the lower levels of the Steel Fortress as if the patrols are its own idea - which they may be. In battle it dips its head and rams with the full force of its neck, and the garrison has learned that a Tuskhog arriving at a run is more reliable early warning than any bell.

Field note: Tuskhog always lowers its head three seconds before charging - experienced trainers use those three seconds to redirect it at their problem instead.

About Ironboar

Ironboar is a fully armoured war-boar with interlocking steel plates covering its flanks, shoulders, and face, two great curved tusks banded in dark iron, and a single prominent horn rising from the centre of its armoured forehead. Its charge shakes the ground fifty metres out. The Steel Fortress has three gates; two of them were reinforced specifically with Ironboar charges in mind. Ironboar holds no grudges - it simply believes the fortress should be exactly as strong as it is, and regularly tests the hypothesis.

Field note: Ironboar has been recorded charging the same outer wall section every spring for twelve years - the garrison now treats it as an annual structural stress test.
