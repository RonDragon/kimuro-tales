About Crowmber

Crowmber is a round, scruffy black crow chick with orange-red glowing eyes that look too large for its small head. Its stubby wings are just long enough to flutter uselessly, and it compensates by hopping vigorously toward anything interesting. At the Lava Crater it perches on cooling obsidian ledges, watching the lava flows with those bright ember-eyes, counting things only it understands. Its feathers are slightly singed at the tips, which seems to be the natural state rather than the result of any accident.

Field note: Crowmber chicks always face the volcano when sleeping - no matter which direction their perch points, they reorient during the night.

About Ravux

Ravux is a full-grown crow with sleek black plumage, wide swept wings edged in ember-orange, and those unnerving orange eyes now sharp and calculating. It sits very upright, turning its head at precise angles to examine things from multiple perspectives before acting. In the Lava Crater it rides thermal columns rising from open lava lakes, gaining altitude effortlessly. When it calls out, the sound carries farther than seems possible for a bird its size - the crater's acoustics amplify its voice into a distant, ringing caw.

Field note: Ravux can locate a thermal column by tilting its head fifteen degrees precisely and sampling the airflow with its crest feathers - it never circles twice before finding one.

About Shadowryx

Shadowryx is a large, imposing dark crow with a broad wingspan edged in deep orange-red like slow-burning coal, heavy tail feathers that trail embers when it flies, and eyes that glow with the steady heat of the Lava Crater's core. It moves deliberately and without unnecessary display - it does not need display. Where it lands, the rock warms visibly. It is one of the few creatures that can cross the crater's active flow zone without deviation, reading the lava's movement patterns in the heat shimmer days before they shift.

Field note: Shadowryx lands on the same three obsidian spires at each sunset in sequence - the spires have been measured to be fractionally warmer than the surrounding rock.
