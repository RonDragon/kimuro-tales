About Sporbin

Sporbin is an adorably round mushroom-cap creature with a pale brown cap and a single glowing blue eye. A soft haze of ghost-energy wisps around its base, faint as morning mist. It bounces through the bug thicket with endearing energy, its cap bobbing with each hop. The ghost element means it occasionally passes partway through a leaf or twig without noticing, then looks startled when it catches what happened. Thicket children follow Sporbin trails because the ghost-haze marks a path through dense undergrowth better than any lantern.

Field note: Sporbin's ghost-wisps turn green when it is happy and blue-white when it is frightened - a reliable mood-indicator every thicket traveler learns.

About Fungling

Fungling has grown into a stooped, root-heavy form - broad dark cap, a gnarled woody body with root-like legs dug into the soil, and purple ghost-mist rising steadily from around its trunk. Smaller mushroom growths dot its shoulders. It moves slowly but with deep purpose, and when it stops, the roots extend into the soil and connect directly to the thicket's mycelium network for hours. Fungling seems to hold conversations with the thicket floor, and the purple mist thickens when it is listening to something old.

Field note: When Fungling's ghost-mist turns deep violet, old thicket explorers start moving quickly in the other direction - they say something ancient is speaking.

About Arborveil

Arborveil is part fungus, part tree, and part ghost - its massive mushroom cap has grown to shade a full clearing, and its root-trunk body has merged with the soil so completely that it is impossible to say where Arborveil ends and the thicket begins. Ghost-light streams upward through every gap in its cap. It is one of the oldest living things in the bug thicket, and it remembers the guardian order from before Abalont's shadow fell. Arborveil does not move; it grows - imperceptibly, relentlessly, through the dark seasons.

Field note: Arborveil's cap grows exactly one centimetre in diameter each year - scholars can date the bug thicket's history by measuring its cap rings.
