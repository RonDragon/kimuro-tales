About Stonecub

Stonecub is a compact, low-slung bear cub with a very dark charcoal-black coat that blends into the shadow cave walls perfectly in dim light. Its small eyes catch light with an orange glint, and its claws are already short and stubby-hard. It spends its days pressing its belly to the cave floor and shuffling forward in near silence - a gait that seems more rock-slide than walk. It tests every stone it encounters by pressing its nose to it for thirty full seconds before moving on. Trainers say this is how Stonecub decides if a thing is worth remembering.

Field note: Stonecub's nose-test lasts exactly thirty seconds for every stone - it has never been caught shortening the count.

About Quarrite

Quarrite has grown massively broad, with jagged rock plates crusting along its spine, shoulders, and haunches like natural armor. Its dark coat shows through between the stone plates in patches, and its claws have lengthened into serious digging tools. It moves through the shadow caves at a determined lumbering pace that shakes loose small stones from the ceiling, and it uses its armored back to clear blocked passages by simply walking into them. It claims the deepest chambers of each cave system and marks the entrance with a ring of stone it has personally bitten out of the wall.

Field note: Quarrite's entrance-stone ring can be identified by its unique tooth-marks - cave researchers use them to map who owns which chamber.

About Geolithaun

Geolithaun is a colossal dark bear that has become half-rock - a ridge of sharp stone spines runs the length of its spine and its entire back is a mountain range in miniature, covered in craggy dark basalt. When it rears up in the shadow caves and roars, the reverberations travel for half a kilometer through solid stone and small stones rattle loose from the ceiling above. It does not charge - it simply becomes an immovable fact of the landscape, and the challenge is accepted or it is not. The oldest cave-drawings show Geolithaun standing watch before the shadow caves were first mapped, and the drawings are very old.

Field note: Geolithaun has not moved from its cave threshold in the three hundred years the cave records cover - researchers argue whether it is sleeping or simply waiting.
