About Hornpod

A round, soft blue-grey narwhal pup with an oversized horn barely longer than a finger and huge guileless eyes. It floats in the light grove's still pools on its round belly, spiraling its tiny horn slowly as if tuning into something. The light grove's reflection pools are said to show the future to those patient enough to wait, and Hornpod drifts toward the brightest reflections without hesitation. Young guardians find it by following the ripples it leaves behind - always in perfect spiral patterns.

Field note: Hornpod's horn spirals create perfectly-spaced ripples of exactly 3 centimetres apart - naturalists have never found an exception.

About Narwhisk

Narwhisk has grown into a graceful, spotted blue-grey cetacean form with a long spiraling horn that glows softly at the tip. It skims the light grove's reflection pools just below the surface, its horn breaking the water in front of it and casting prismatic light across the pool-bed. It has developed its psychic reading of the water's surface - ripples carry information to it about events upstream and down, both in water and in time. Guardians who seek Narwhisk in the grove say it always knows they are coming before they arrive.

Field note: Narwhisk surfaces in the exact spot where the next guardian will approach the pool - it has never been recorded swimming to the wrong side.

About Spiralux

Spiralux is magnificent - a large blue-grey narwhal form with swirling lore-patterns etched in its skin like carved ivory. Its great spiral horn catches the light grove's light and refracts it into complex patterns that move across the grove floor. Ancient. Slow. Infinitely patient. The light grove was built around the pools it inhabits, and the pools were dug by its own passage. When Spiralux sings - a rare event - the light grove becomes briefly visible from every other region at once, as a distant shimmer on the horizon.

Field note: Spiralux's song is audible once per decade, for exactly eleven minutes - grove scholars have kept records for 200 years, and it has never been twelve.
