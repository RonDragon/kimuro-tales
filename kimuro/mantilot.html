About Mantilot

Round and flat as a sand dollar, Mantilot ripples its wide sand-coloured wings to glide just above the desert floor. Its large round eyes are set wide apart, and a pattern of pale spots dots its broad back like a star chart. It skims the sand dunes at dawn, leaving curving trails that locals call 'sky-lines' - each one a different shape as if the little ray is drawing. The sand dunes were quieter before the guardians arrived; Mantilot only draws near when it hears a voice it trusts.

Field note: Mantilot's sand-skimming trails always curve in the same direction as the wind, never against it - it rides every gust it finds.

About Manticore

Manticore's body has grown into a flattened diamond shape with swept-back wingtips and a long slender tail. Sandy-brown with faint golden markings, it cruises two hand-spans above the dune surface, never touching down except to rest. Its psychic sense lets it feel the shape of hidden objects - buried stones, seeds, sometimes lost guardian tools - and it circles above them, hovering, as if pointing them out. The dune-folk learned to follow it when they'd lost something important.

Field note: When Manticore circles a spot three times without landing, something valuable lies exactly 30 centimeters beneath the sand.

About Mantabyss

Mantabyss is massive and unhurried, its wide dark-gold wings spanning as wide as a doorway on each side. Ancient rune-markings in deep ochre stripe its back - scholars argue whether they are natural pigment or something older, written into the very skin. It drifts above the deepest dunes at night, and those who watch say its body shimmers with the outline of buried ruins beneath the sand. The guardians believe Mantabyss remembers the desert before the dunes existed.

Field note: Three times per century, Mantabyss descends to the lowest dune valley and traces a pattern in the sand that takes three days to uncover fully.
