About Capkid

Capkid is a small white goat with a cream-coloured coat and two small nubbin horns still growing in. It picks its way across the Sand Dunes on delicate hooves with total confidence, tilting its head at every new rock formation as though cataloguing them for later. It can balance on a stone no bigger than a fist and seems to regard gravity as a mild suggestion. Dune caravanners say a Capkid sighted above the ridgeline means solid footing lies beneath - it never walks where the sand will shift.

Field note: Capkid taps the ground three times with a forehoof before stepping onto new terrain - experienced dune guides do the same after watching long enough.

About Caphorn

Caphorn has grown into a sturdy white-grey mountain goat with long curling horns and a serious amber gaze. Its legs have thickened and its hooves now have a hard mineral edge from years of climbing sandstone. It claims the highest dune ridges and surveys everything below from a favoured perch, utterly untroubled by the wind. Caphorn marks its territory not with scent but by scoring its horns across rock - the scratch-marks are as distinctive as a signature, and no other creature has ever tried to erase one.

Field note: Caphorn's horn-marks on rock have been found up to forty kilometres apart, suggesting it covers far more dune country in a season than anyone expected.

About Stonecrown

Stonecrown wears its crown well - its massive layered stone-plated back rises like a ridge of small peaks, and its sweeping multi-branched horns are thick enough to be used as handholds by lost climbers. The rocky plating on its haunches and shoulders is actual compressed desert stone fused into its coat over a lifetime of dune crossings. It stands at the highest points of the Sand Dunes and, when it calls out across the ridgelines, the resonance carries for miles - a signal that has guided lost travellers home since before the oldest maps were drawn.

Field note: The stone plates on Stonecrown's back develop unique crystal inclusions over decades - no two adults have the same pattern, and elder ones have been read like geological records.
