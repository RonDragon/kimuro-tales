About Tinwhyx

Tinwhyx is a pale lavender-white narwhal pup with a single gold-spiraling horn and glowing swirl markings on its round body. Its large eyes carry a faint violet glow. It drifts in the coral bay's shallows, horn-first, using its psychic sense to taste the emotions of the coral formations - happy coral, stressed coral, growing coral. It produces small rings of light around its horn when it finds something that pleases it. Bay-keepers follow Tinwhyx to find the healthiest coral gardens.

Field note: Tinwhyx's light-rings appear only above coral that is actively growing - it is a more accurate reef-health indicator than any instrument.

About Spirwhyx

Spirwhyx has grown sleek and graceful, its lavender-blue body now adorned with deeper violet spiral markings that pulse when it uses its psychic abilities. Its horn is longer and catches tides of emotional energy from the coral bay's entire reef system. It drifts at moderate depth, occasionally surfacing to release a spray of luminous droplets that hang in the air before dissolving. The droplets spell different shapes each time - local legend holds each shape corresponds to a current in an ancient ocean map.

Field note: Spirwhyx's spray-patterns have been documented for 60 years; scholars have identified 214 distinct spray-shapes so far.

About Maelwyx

Maelwyx is a creature of genuine grandeur - enormous, white and violet, its body half-submerged in a permanent swirling current it creates simply by existing. Its long horn crackles with psychic energy visible as violet light, and ice-crystal formations ring the horn's base where psychic and water energy collide. It can read the entire coral bay at once - every creature, every current, every reef crack. Old records say Maelwyx was the coral bay's original guardian, appointed before Abalont's age, and that it has been keeping the reef growing through every dark season since.

Field note: Whenever Maelwyx surfaces fully, the coral bay's water temperature drops by 2 degrees and the fish count in the adjacent grid squares doubles within the hour.
