About Carapup

Carapup is a small, determined-looking tortoise with a neat domed grey shell and a slightly pouty expression. Its four solid legs end in blunt-clawed toes perfect for ploughing through sand without sinking. It plods across the Sand Dunes at its chosen pace - which is slow - and regards faster-moving creatures with calm suspicion. Dune children chase Carapup but never catch it; it simply walks in a straight line until the child tires, then turns around and plods past them again.

Field note: Carapup maintains exactly the same walking pace regardless of terrain - sand, rock, or slope, measured at precisely 0.3 km/h over a full kilometre.

About Caramax

Caramax is a wide, heavy tortoise whose shell has developed a ridged texture and amber-brown scutes that blend with the dune landscape. Its broad flat head carries a furrowed brow that seems to express permanent mild thought. It has learned to read the sand by the way it shifts around its feet - it never walks into a dune slide and never climbs a slope it cannot descend. Dune caravanners use Caramax route-lines as safe-passage maps; the paths it chooses are still there decades later.

Field note: Caramax's shell pattern exactly mirrors the dune stripe formations of its home region - researchers believe it actively seeks terrain that matches its own colouration.

About Caracolossus

Caracolossus is ancient and enormous - its shell is a geography of ridged stone segments, with golden-amber scutes worn smooth by centuries of sand and each leg as thick as a pillar. Its slow, rhythmic footsteps send tremors through the dune in advance of its arrival; the sand shifts to accommodate it. Sand Dunes tradition holds that Caracolossus walked a single great circuit of the region before the first settlement was built, and it has been walking the same route ever since - a living boundary marker for everything the dunes contain.

Field note: Caracolossus completes its circuit of Sand Dunes in exactly forty-two days regardless of obstacles - researchers have documented it walking into and out of flooded sections without changing pace.
