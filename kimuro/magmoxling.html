About Magmoxling

Magmoxling is a small solid rhinoceros cub with dark grey-brown hide showing early cracks of orange where future lava-channels will grow. Its single short horn is more of a bump than a weapon yet, and lava-lines trace the joins of its hide-plates in thin glowing seams. It navigates Dragon Spire's rock-strewn lower approaches with stubby confidence, headbutting every stone it encounters - not to move them, but to test them. Guardians on long climbs find Magmoxling useful company; it always finds the most stable footing first.

Field note: Magmoxling headbutts rocks to test their density - it has never once picked a path that later collapsed.

About Magmoxhorn

Magmoxhorn is built like a basalt wall that chose to walk. Its wide body is plated in overlapping grey-brown rock-armor with lava-orange cracks running between every plate, and its long curved horn has grown substantial. Dragon Spire's middle slopes are its range, and it establishes position early - choosing a section of the path and holding it with its mass alone. Other creatures navigate around it. Its armor-plates groan like shifting stone in high wind, and the sound echoes down the Spire's stone gullies as a low warning.

Field note: Magmoxhorn always faces the Spire's active vents - when it turns away, veteran climbers know a vent is about to exhaust itself.

About Magmoxvern

Magmoxvern is as close to a living fortress as Dragon Spire produces. Enormously broad, its entire back is a landscape of fused rock-plates and open lava-channels, with spikes of cooling basalt rising like battlements along its spine. Three horns crown its head in a forward-pointing trident. It does not climb Dragon Spire - it forms part of it; researchers have confused sections of Magmoxvern's stationary back for natural rock formations. Only the lava-glow shifting between its plates reveals the difference. It is said it has guarded the Spire's summit passage since before the guardians arrived.

Field note: Three times each year Magmoxvern exhales hard enough to clear the summit pass of ash buildup - climbers plan their ascents around this schedule.
