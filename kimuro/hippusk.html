About Hippusk

Tuskos's first form is a squat, grey-brown creature with the broad face and wide nostrils of a hippo, stumpy legs, and a beginning set of small forward-facing tusks just emerging. Its hide is already thick and slightly rough to the touch. It sits exactly where it is placed and looks at everything with an expression of extreme patience that seems implausible for something this small. In the Crystal Mines it waddles into crystal-lit caverns and simply sits, which other creatures interpret as wisdom - it is mostly just comfortable.

Field note: Young Tuskos calves can sit perfectly still for two hours - researchers initially mistook them for unusually convincing stone carvings.

About Tuskodon

Tuskodon is enormously wide and low, its thick grey hide now studded with rock-crystal protrusions that catch light from every direction. Its two large forward tusks have become thick and blunt-edged, excellent for prying up crystal formations from the mine floor - which it does regularly and seemingly at random. It moves through narrow mine passages by simply expanding them, and the Crystal Mines maintenance crews have learned to re-route paths around Tuskodon's preferred corridors rather than fight it.

Field note: Tuskodon always yawns before it moves - the yawn functions as a ten-second warning system, which the mine crews genuinely rely on.

About Megatus

Megatus is a colossal, open-mouthed behemoth - all wide jaw and mountainous stone-hide - whose body has gradually fused with the crystal and rock of the mines over decades. Crystal formations now grow directly from its spine and shoulders, glowing with the same light as the deepest Crystal Mine veins. Its roar can shatter loose crystal formations fifty metres away. Megatus does not guard the mines - it simply is the mines, as much as any tunnel or vein, and has been here longer than the first miners' torches.

Field note: Crystal formations grow on Megatus's back at a measurable rate - geologists use them to date the creature's age to at least two hundred years.
