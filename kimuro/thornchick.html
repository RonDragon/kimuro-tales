About Thornchick

A tiny bipedal wyvern hatchling with a dusty olive-green body, a single blunt party-hat horn on its head, and a round green spot on its chest like a leaf-badge, Thornchick totters around the Dark Forest floor squawking at falling leaves. Its little forearms are nearly vestigial and it balances with a comically intense expression. The horn is harmless for now but grows with remarkable speed - forest elders have measured a Thornchick's horn extending three millimetres in a single day. Dark Forest saplings grow faster in the patches where Thornchick sleeps, apparently benefiting from something it exhales at night.

Field note: Saplings in a five-metre radius of a Thornchick's sleeping spot grow measurably faster than those outside it - the mechanism is not understood.

About Thornwing

Thornwing has grown into an elegant pterosaur-form with broad membranous wings edged in vivid pink-purple veining, and two elegant curved head-crests that sweep back like a compass rose. Its leaf-patterned wings beat in slow arcing circles over the Dark Forest canopy, and the undersides catch forest light to flash the wing-veins in brilliant colour. It rides thermal columns above the treeline and calls downward in spiraling glides, letting the forest guide its path. Dark Forest hunters use Thornwing circling patterns as a map of where air-columns are rising - it reliably marks forest clearings from above.

Field note: Thornwing circles exactly three times before landing, always completing the third circle even if clearly tired - the pattern never varies.

About Thornodactyl

Thornodactyl is a formidable wyvern-dragon of the Dark Forest - a great dark-green scaled beast with enormous crystal-pink wing-spines, powerful hind legs, and a body armoured in overlapping scale-plates. Its head bears a full crown-array of crystal-blade horns, and its tail ends in a heavy scythe-tip. When Thornodactyl spreads its full wingspan above the Dark Forest canopy, it creates a brief silence - every bird, bug, and smaller creature holds absolutely still until the shadow passes. Ancient Dark Forest lore says Thornodactyl and the forest struck a peace: it does not burn the canopy, and the canopy shelters its eggs.

Field note: Dark Forest fires have never spread to the two square kilometres around Thornodactyl's nesting site - investigators found no structural difference in the trees, only the creature's presence.
