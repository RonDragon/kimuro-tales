About Embrowl

Embrowl is a tiny fluffy white-grey owlet so round it looks more like a snowball with eyes than a bird. Its large glowing orange eyes dominate its face, and small ember-sparks drift from its downy feathers at random - it cannot fully control the embers yet, which means it sets fire to small things near it when startled. Found on the low sheltered ledges of storm spire, it presses itself against warm rock and watches the world with unblinking orange intensity. Children at the spire say Embrowl is easy to find at night because it looks like a floating pair of orange lamp-eyes.

Field note: Embrowl's ember-sparks are harmless at stage one, but they trace tiny shapes in the air that fade before anyone can make them out.

About Flamstryx

Flamstryx has shed its white down for vivid orange-gold feathers, and a crown of flame now burns permanently from the crest of its head. Its body is lean and powerful - more hawk than owl now - and its wings beat with a sharp crackle audible at twenty meters. It hunts the storm spire's wind-corridors where prey is scarce and skill is everything, learning to time its strikes between gust-pulses. The spire guides say Flamstryx can ride a vertical air column standing completely still, simply watching, for six hours without landing.

Field note: Flamstryx's fire-crest changes color in different winds: orange in warm drafts, deep red in cold ones, white in storm-core gusts.

About Infernus

Infernus is a great golden-red bird of the highest spire peaks, wreathed in orange flame that streams from every primary feather as a living fire-trail when it flies. When it spreads its wings the thermal column beneath it heats fast enough to be felt on the ground far below. Its roar crosses the full spire valley. Ancient storm-spire maps show Infernus in the same position at the summit - wings spread, fire blazing - suggesting it has occupied the peak for longer than records exist. The storm that gave the spire its name was, by some accounts, Infernus deciding to stretch.

Field note: Infernus leaves exactly seven ember trails in the sky during any single flight; never six, never eight.
