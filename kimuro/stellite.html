About Stellite

No bigger than a fist, Stellite is a tiny pale-blue creature with a faceted crystal dome on its back and two small antennae poking up from its rounded head. It skitters sideways across wet frost-rock, its shell catching the light and scattering tiny rainbows. When startled, it tucks its antennae flat and becomes almost indistinguishable from a chip of blue ice. Young Stellites sometimes wash into the meltpools of Frozen Peaks and stay for months, polishing their shells on the grit-rough stones.

Field note: Each Stellite's shell facets are unique - no two scatter light in the same pattern.

About Stellox

Stellox has grown a broad rounded shell covered in sparkling ice-crystal facets that catch every angle of light. Its body is pale blue-white and it moves with a slow, deliberate grace along the frozen shallows. The shell is not merely decoration - it holds enough cold meltwater inside to keep its temperature stable for days away from the ice. Stellox leaves faint frost trails on sun-warmed rock, and herders use those glittering marks to read a cold front before the storms arrive.

Field note: Frost trails left by Stellox on morning rock correctly predicted thirty-seven cold fronts in Frozen Peaks last year.

About Stellaryx

Stellaryx's shell has erupted into a dense crown of ice-blue crystal spikes that jut outward at every angle, each one sharp enough to split a wave. The creature itself is wide and low, moving with the patient slowness of a glacier. When it submerges in the meltwater shallows of Frozen Peaks, only the spike-tips show above the waterline - from a distance they look like strange sea urchins. Those who disturb Stellaryx hear a grinding crack of shifting ice before they see anything at all.

Field note: Stellaryx's spike-tips freeze a thin layer of seawater overnight - by dawn each spike has grown by one finger-width.
