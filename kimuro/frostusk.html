About Frostusk

Frostusk is the most cheerful thing in Frozen Peaks - a round fluffy mammoth calf with wide blue eyes, a short curving trunk, and two small tusks just beginning to grow. Its thick brown wool is already long enough to drag slightly in the snow. It follows the range's snowline at first light, using its trunk to sniff at every wind-stranded twig and ice fragment, occasionally rolling a particularly interesting one all the way back to show its companions. Guardians new to Frozen Peaks always meet Frostusk first, because it finds new arrivals before they find anything else.

Field note: Frostusk collects round pebbles - always exactly round, never oval - and arranges them in lines nobody has worked out the reason for.

About Mammox

Mammox has grown into the full weight of a mammoth - great thick wool over a massive body, long curved tusks that each end in a faint frost-tip, and feet wide enough to leave deep circular prints in wet beach sand. It still walks the tide line, but now its presence changes what it finds there: the water around its feet drops half a degree in temperature, and small ice-crystals sometimes form on the wet sand behind its steps. Guardians use Mammox to keep Frozen Peaks' fresh-water springs from overheating in summer.

Field note: Mammox's tusks accumulate a thin frost layer at their tips at night - by morning the frost has sublimated, but the air near them smells of glacier.

About Glacimoth

Glacimoth is ancient and immense - a woolly mammoth of near-mythic scale, its thick brown-grey coat frosted at every tip, its long sweeping tusks each trailing visible ice-crystal plumes in cold air. A jagged crest of ice-spines runs the ridge of its back from shoulder to hindquarters. The ground beneath its feet is always frozen to a depth of a hand's width, and it moves at the pace of a glacier - deliberately, without urgency, reshaping the land as it passes. Frozen Peaks' oldest legends say Glacimoth chose the peaks because the water helps it remember the cold world it came from, and that when it lowers its tusks to the water, the whole bay runs a little clearer.

Field note: Glacimoth's footprints remain frozen for up to three days after it passes - the deepest ones have been used as water-troughs by other creatures.
