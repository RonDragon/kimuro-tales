About Thornyx

A compact, bright-green lizard-shaped creature entirely covered in layered leaf-spines that make it look like a walking pile of fresh foliage, Thornyx forages across the Bug Thicket floor using its thick spiked tail for balance. Its big round eyes are leaf-green and alert, and its leaf-quills are supple enough to bend around branches without snagging. It sits motionless among actual leaves to vanish entirely - the only tell is the faint curl of its tail tip. Bug Thicket young are taught to count the leaves in a bush carefully before sitting on it, because of Thornyx.

Field note: Thornyx is camouflaged well enough that three Bug Thicket survey teams failed to spot one sitting on an open path in full daylight.

About Quillhog

Quillhog has grown into a muscular four-legged lizard with a full spine-mane of earth-tone quills - green at the tips, brown and orange mid-shaft - and a long sweeping tail ringed with spine-clusters. It moves through the Bug Thicket with a low, deliberate prowl, pressing its belly close to the ground when hunting and rising onto stiffened legs when defending. The quills have become properly weaponised now: a full body-shake releases a shower of barbed spine-tips into the air around it. Thicket insects give it a wide berth, which suits Quillhog perfectly.

Field note: Quillhog's body-shake defense fires spine-tips in a two-metre radius - it can replace its full quill-coat in approximately thirty hours.

About Fernarch

Fernarch is a great armoured lizard-behemoth whose body has merged with the Bug Thicket itself - its spines have grown so long and tangled with actual living vines that it now carries a full canopy of ferns and small plants on its back as it moves. Its four legs are pillar-thick, and its tread opens furrows in the thicket floor that quickly become waterways. Fernarch moves slowly, eats constantly, and has remodelled more of the Bug Thicket's terrain than any other creature. Ancient thicket records call Fernarch the Gardener-Who-Walks - every major fern-grove in the thicket began as a spine-plant dropped from Fernarch's back.

Field note: Every major fern-grove in the Bug Thicket has been traced to a location where Fernarch rested; the spines it sheds take root and grow.
