About Chillpeep

Chillpeep is an impossibly round grey-and-white penguin chick with a tiny beak, one bright blue eye showing, and a belly it rests on more than its feet. It rolled off a ledge on its first day and discovered it preferred rolling to walking, which has not changed. Found on the lower Frozen Peaks ice-shelves, it spends most of its day sliding down the same icy slope and waddling back up, apparently convinced that this is the most important work a creature can do. The guardians of the Peaks keep a count of its runs - the current record is 347 in a day.

Field note: Chillpeep leaves a different-shaped tummy-slide print each run; no two recorded impressions have ever matched.

About Frostpenguin

Frostpenguin is a dignified, upright emperor penguin with a sleek black-and-white body and a bold yellow throat-patch. It stands at the edge of the high Frozen Peaks ledges watching the cloud-sea below with calm authority. It no longer rolls. It disapproves of rolling. When the storm-winds begin to build, it is the first to know and it calls out a single measured honk that carries across the entire Peaks - other creatures freeze, then run for shelter, then the storm arrives. It has never been early and it has never been late.

Field note: Frostpenguin's single storm-warning call is the most reliable weather forecast in the entire region - never off by more than ninety seconds.

About Glacemperor

Glacemperor is an armoured warrior penguin - a tall, powerful bird with great ice-blue wing-blades fanning out like frozen armour panels, and a striking face marked with bold blue-and-gold patterning above its sharp beak. The ice-wings do not help it fly; instead they catch wind to help it surf storm-fronts at extraordinary speed. When a true gale sweeps Frozen Peaks, Glacemperor runs directly into it, beak-first, and rides the storm's edge clean around the mountain in a matter of minutes. The guardians call it the Gale Marshal.

Field note: A Glacemperor riding a storm-front has been clocked at 115 km/h - faster than the wind driving it.
