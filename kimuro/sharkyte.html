About Sharkyte

Sharkyte is a small upright-standing shark with dark steel-blue scales, stubby arms that end in clawed fins, and big determined red eyes. Its small dorsal fin is rigid as sheet metal. It waddles across the shallows of Coral Bay on its tail-base with surprising confidence, inspecting anything metallic it finds on the seafloor and collecting it into small piles near its favourite rocks. Trainers say it is deeply sincere - if Sharkyte decides you are its friend, nothing in the bay will bother you.

Field note: Sharkyte's metallic fin rings like a tuning fork when struck - reef musicians tune to it.

About Sharkonyx

Sharkonyx has grown into a broad-chested shark-quadruped with thick plated legs and a reinforced snout that it uses to batter through coral formations like a battering ram. Its dark blue scales have hardened into steel-edged plates that shed water in sheets. Sharkonyx patrols the deep coral channels of Coral Bay with methodical precision, cataloguing the reef in sections the way a careful scholar catalogues words. The guardians valued it as a reef engineer - passages it cleared are still used as the safest routes today.

Field note: The passages Sharkonyx clears through coral grow back around the edges in perfect arches - local divers call them steel gates.

About Gigashark

The final form is colossal - Gigashark sprawls wide with an enormous plated body, four massive armoured limbs, and a head the size of a small boat, studded with protruding steel spines along the jaw ridge. Its dark blue steel-plated hide has fused into a single continuous armour. When it moves through the deep water of Coral Bay, the surface above shows its wake like a moving island. It is the bay's oldest guardian and the reef has slowly grown around it - coral polyps colonise its back fins as though they are just another kind of reef.

Field note: Coral has grown so thick on Gigashark's dorsal fins that small fish nest there without realising what the rock they live on is.
