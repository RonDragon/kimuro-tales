About Leafscale

Leafscale is a compact little lizard-dragon with bright green scales, a short leaf-tipped tail, and stubby legs that carry it in a quick excitable trot through the Bug Thicket undergrowth. A small ridge of leaf-shaped spines runs from its crown to its tail. Its wide eyes track the constant movement of the Thicket's insects and it has developed a hunting technique of absolute stillness followed by a single lunging snap - it catches flying insects out of midair with a success rate that larger, faster predators cannot match.

Field note: Leafscale has been timed catching an airborne insect in forty-three milliseconds - faster than most predators twice its size.

About Verdyx

Verdyx is a well-built medium-sized lizard-dragon with broad leaf-thorn spines running down its back and flanks, a heavy armoured head, and a sweeping leaf-edged tail it uses both as a rudder while climbing and a weapon in territory disputes. Its green scales have darkened and it navigates the Bug Thicket by scent and vibration - it holds its head low and its tongue flickers constantly. The Thicket's insect colonies have learned to treat Verdyx's approach as a neutral event, which only encourages it to walk through their trails at full speed.

Field note: Verdyx walks through the Bug Thicket's most active ant highways without detouring - the ants part, close behind it, and continue as if nothing happened.

About Phyllodrak

Phyllodrak is a powerful dragon whose broad armoured body is covered in layered leaf-scale plates and flanked by great spiked leaf-wings it can spread to block out the sun above the Bug Thicket canopy. Its back ridge of spines is now enormous - each spine the length of an arm - and its tail is heavy with leaf-armour plating. It rests at the deepest heart of the Thicket where the insect population is richest, motionless for days at a time, before suddenly moving the whole colony on a moment's silent judgment. Nothing in the Thicket challenges it. The largest beetles leave offerings at its resting spots.

Field note: The beetles that nest nearest to Phyllodrak's resting sites are consistently thirty percent larger than those in other parts of the Thicket - researchers attribute this to soil enrichment.
