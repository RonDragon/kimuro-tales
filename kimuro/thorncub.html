About Thorncub

A fluffy, round moss-green bear cub with a single pale leaf-thorn sprouting from the top of its head, Thorncub toddles around the Dragon Spire's mossy base levels snuffling at every crevice. Its vivid green coat catches the Spire's filtered light and makes it look like a walking bush - an effect Thorncub seems to use deliberately by sitting among ferns when it wants to rest undisturbed. It loves to press its single head-thorn against things to test their hardness, and the thorn is already stronger than it looks. The Spire's older guardians say Thorncub always knows where the hidden exits are.

Field note: Thorncub's head-thorn test-prods every new surface it encounters - dragon-scale, stone, wood, and guardian boots have all received equal investigation.

About Thornbear

Thornbear has grown broad and imposing, its mossy-green coat now shot through with red-tipped spine-thorns that stand erect when it is threatened - turning a rumpled bear into a pincushion-obstacle that nothing wants to rush. It moves through the Dragon Spire with a rolling, confident gait, stopping to excavate moss and fungi from rock faces with strong curved claws. Despite looking fierce, Thornbear has a precise, calm quality to its movements - it only raises its thorns when the situation genuinely warrants it. Dragon Spire hatchlings have been documented sleeping against Thornbear's thorned flank, apparently trusting the signal system completely.

Field note: Thornbear's spines have been documented standing erect a full three seconds before any human-perceptible threat appears - it registers danger earlier than any nearby guardian.

About Thornothyr

Thornothyr is a great dark-shelled bear-titan whose body has grown so large and still that fungi and lichen colonise the flat surfaces between its massive thorns. Its back is covered in overlapping dark-brown thorn-plates sharp enough to split stone, and its jaw can crush granite. It walks the Dragon Spire's deepest fissures with the unhurried weight of something that has never needed to run. When Thornothyr stands on its rear legs - which it does when truly angry - it blocks the entire passage it is in. Ancient Spire records describe it as the wall between the Spire's fissures and whatever is behind them.

Field note: Lichen colonies on Thornothyr's back are taxonomically different from any found elsewhere in the Spire - they have only been found growing on this creature.
