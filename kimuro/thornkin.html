About Thornkin

A bright green quadruped colt with a fluffy leaf-tufted tail and a ruff of short yellow-tipped spines along its neck, Thornkin scrambles up Granite Heights' lower rock shelves with an enthusiasm that outpaces its coordination. Its legs are slightly too long for its body, which leads to endearing stumbles quickly recovered with a proud shake of its spiny mane. It grazes on the thin patches of grass between granite slabs and can identify edible lichen from twenty paces. Young guardians at the Heights are taught to follow a Thornkin to find safe routes - it has never led one off a cliff.

Field note: Thornkin marks safe footing by pawing granite once; it marks loose gravel with two rapid scrapes - every young creature in the Heights has learned the code.

About Thornaxe

Thornaxe has grown into a powerful, lean four-legged beast with flowing bright-green and gold-yellow mane-hackles along its neck and spine, and a body built for the Granite Heights' uneven terrain. It leaps boulder-to-boulder with startling precision, and its rear hooves leave small chips in the rock face at each takeoff. The spines along its back are now full-length and serrated at the tips - they catch the highland wind and hum faintly when facing into the breeze. Explorers say following a Thornaxe is the fastest safe way through the Heights because it has every path memorised.

Field note: Thornaxe's back-spines hum at a distinct pitch when the wind comes from the east - highland farmers call the sound 'the Heights speaking' and plan planting by it.

About Thornolith

Thornolith stands at the highest granite peak of the Heights, its dark green and gold body powerful and battle-scarred, its mane of layered leaf-spines grown into a magnificent array that fans wide in the mountain wind. Great rock-plate growths armour its shoulder joints. When it calls across the peaks, the sound seems to come from the granite itself - the rock carries and amplifies it in all directions. Ancient guardian texts name Thornolith the Heights' Compass: every explorer who has been lost on the Granite Heights and survived has described finding their way by following Thornolith's call back to safety.

Field note: Thornolith's territorial call has been measured to carry thirty kilometres on a still day - it is the first sound heard by anyone arriving at the Heights' northern pass.
