About Gemlite

Gemlite is a small stocky grey rhinoceros calf with a single short horn and wide blue glowing eyes that see slightly into the infrared spectrum, letting it navigate the Crystal Mines in complete darkness. Its smooth grey hide is just beginning to show the faintest raised nodules where future crystal growth will emerge. Despite its small size it is remarkably dense, and attempting to push it when it doesn't want to move is like trying to push a small boulder. It stamps its forehoof twice when pleased and three times when it wants someone to pay attention - trainers learn this quickly.

Field note: Gemlite can locate any gem vein in a thirty-meter radius by pressing its horn to the wall and standing silent for exactly thirty seconds.

About Amethorne

Amethorne has grown into a proper rhinoceros with a significant difference: rows of jagged purple amethyst crystals now erupt from its back and sides, catching the mine lantern light and throwing violet patterns across the walls. Its single horn has lengthened and darkened, and its eyes glow a deeper blue. When it charges through a tunnel, the crystal spikes on its flanks score the walls and leave a trail of sparkling purple scratches that miners follow to find new gem seams. Amethorne charges for the joy of it, selecting the longest straight tunnel it can find.

Field note: The purple crystals on Amethorne's back are harder than the surrounding mine walls - they have never been scratched despite thousands of narrow tunnel passages.

About Gemnoceros

Gemnoceros is the apex creature of the Crystal Mines - a massive armored rhinoceros whose entire back, shoulders, and flanks are covered in towering clusters of purple and violet crystal formations that have grown into a full natural armor. Rune-like markings of pale blue are etched into the hide between crystal clusters, each one a psychic sigil that focuses its immense mental power. When Gemnoceros stands in the central mine chamber and focuses, it can draw every loose gem in the caves toward itself with a single exhalation. The mines belong to it the way a meadow belongs to its oldest tree.

Field note: Gemnoceros has been in the Crystal Mines so long that the largest crystal formations grew outward from its sleeping spots - the mines themselves were shaped by its rest.
