About Blazkit

Blazkit is a small blue armadillo with a rounded shell and a tuft of yellow spines along its back that stick out like a crown it hasn't grown into yet. It trundles across the Granite Heights with a rolling gait, tapping the rock with its blunt snout to listen for hollow spaces beneath. When it finds one, it marks the spot with a drool-print - an indispensable skill that the heights' miners quietly rely on. Blazkit rolls into a tight ball when startled but immediately unrolls to check if the danger was interesting enough to deserve a better look.

Field note: Blazkit's snout-tap can locate a hollow rock cavity down to three meters - the Heights' miners follow its trail before drilling.

About Blazorm

Blazorm's shell has thickened into a heavy ridged dome the color of polished granite, and its yellow crest-spines have grown broad and flat like shields. It walks with its head low, powerful arms swinging, and can overturn a two-meter boulder without breaking its stride. The Granite Heights' communities know Blazorm by the perfectly smooth bowl-shaped depressions it leaves when it rests - pressed into solid rock. It does not sleep lightly; once it settles, not even a thunderstorm will wake it before it is ready.

Field note: Blazorm's resting-bowl impressions in granite are smooth as polished marble - climbers use them as handholds on the sheer heights.

About Terrablazer

Terrablazer has grown so large and heavy that it no longer bothers walking overland - it bulldozes directly through the Granite Heights' stone, tunneling with the slow unstoppable force of a glacier. Its massive shell is now boulder-grey and its wide yellow spines flatten into plates as it plows forward. The Heights' surface is mapped in part by Terrablazer's tunnel network; whole communities have built over its passages without knowing. It surfaces once each season to check the sky, then disappears again, leaving a crater-like depression behind.

Field note: Terrablazer's seasonal surfacing craters are precise enough that the Granite Heights' farmers use them as irrigation channels.
