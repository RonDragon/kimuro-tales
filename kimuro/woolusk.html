About Woolusk

A round, shaggy-brown little mammoth with oversized round ears, big blue eyes, and two tiny curved tusks just beginning to show. Its fluffy coat is the warm russet of baked desert clay. Woolusk snuffles through amber sand looking for buried roots, and stamps a tiny forehoof whenever it finds one - a celebration so vigorous it topples over about half the time. Young guardians crossing the dunes say a tumbling Woolusk is a sign the next day's crossing will be easy.

Field note: Woolusk's tusks are exactly the length of its own stubby nose - it measures them daily by pressing them together.

About Mammoxen

Now broad-shouldered and sturdy, Mammoxen carries a thick ridged shell-plate across its back like hammered amber stone - a natural armour grown from mineral deposits in the desert soil it feeds on. Its two long curved tusks scoop sand aside with practiced ease, revealing hidden water-roots. Each tusk tip glows faintly golden at dusk. Mammoxen herds travel the amber desert in slow circuits following ancient paths the ground itself remembers.

Field note: The ridge-plate on Mammox's back rings like struck sandstone when flicked - wanderers use it to signal one another across the dunes.

About Primammoth

Primammoth is a living sandstone monument - its body broad as a mesa, tusks arching wide as doorframes, its armoured shell-back etched by centuries of wind into ridge-patterns that scholars study like maps. Its footfalls shake the amber desert floor, and buried memory-stones rumble to the surface in its wake. Ancient stories say Primammoth walked the dunes before Abalont cast his shadow, and its tusks still carry grooves from marks carved by the first guardians.

Field note: Scholars claim that tracing the wind-etched grooves on Primammoth's back reveals the shape of the oldest marks in the Guardian sigil-craft.
