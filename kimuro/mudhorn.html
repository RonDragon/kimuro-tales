About Mudhorn

A round, powder-blue dragon-hatchling with a cream-coloured belly disc, stubby horn nubs, and an expression of permanent cheerful bewilderment, Mudhorn sits near the Lava Crater's cooler rim pools and blows small mud-bubbles with its nose. Its little forearms are stubby and expressive, and it slaps the muddy rim with them when excited. Despite the volcano's danger, Mudhorn is entirely unbothered - it has found a warm mud-puddle beside a cooling lava shelf and considers this the perfect home. Guardian lore says Mudhorn was the first creature to figure out that lava cools into useful rock, and has been sitting nearby feeling proud ever since.

Field note: Mudhorn blows exactly one bubble at a time and waits for it to pop before blowing another - it has never been seen to blow two at once.

About Thormud

Thormud is a compact upright dragon brute, dark navy-blue with a broad tan belly and rows of stubby earth-brown dorsal spines running up its back. Its arms are thick now, its posture solid and square, and its horn nubs have grown into proper curved points. It lurches between crater pools with heavy bipedal steps, scooping cooling lava-mud to pack onto its spines - apparently for temperature regulation, though it looks like it is simply proud of being messy. Its deep rumbling vocalizations echo off the crater walls at all hours.

Field note: Thormud re-packs mud onto its own spines approximately every ninety minutes - researchers have timed this to within five minutes without exception.

About Magmamoth

Magmamoth is a titan of the Lava Crater - jet-black hide that has absorbed centuries of volcanic heat, a broad spiked back of dark volcanic rock-points, and a belly still carrying the tan-warm colour of cooling lava. Red-orange ember eyes glow steadily in the crater smoke. It stands completely still for hours at crater's edge, watching the lava flow, and the crater's regular creatures have learned to sleep near it for warmth and protection. Ancient crater lore says Magmamoth and the volcano struck a bargain: the volcano stays where it is, and Magmamoth stays nearby to keep it company.

Field note: Magmamoth's body temperature has been recorded at 58 degrees Celsius at the surface - it runs ten degrees hotter than the warmest recorded lava-rim rock.
