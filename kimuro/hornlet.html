About Hornlet

Hornlet is a round, solid green rhino calf with a broad flat face, a small knobbed horn just emerging from its nose, and a look of cheerful stubborn determination. Its thick legs plant firmly on Granite Heights' rocky ground, and it has already developed the habit of testing every boulder it encounters by pressing its forehead against it - seemingly checking whether it will move. It does not move boulders yet. But it is absolutely clear it intends to work up to that.

Field note: Hornlet spends up to twenty minutes a day pressing its forehead against the same specific boulder - researchers believe it is forming a bond with a chosen landmark.

About Cerathorn

Cerathorn has grown into a formidable creature - its green hide is now armoured with raised stone-ridged scales, and three distinct horns crown its wide face, the largest curving upward in a solid arch. It moves with a heavy-footed confidence that causes small rock slides wherever the path is loose. It charges rarely, but when it does, the vibration is felt before the creature is seen. Granite Heights pathfinders mark their toughest trails by whether Cerathorn uses them regularly - its routes are the most structurally sound.

Field note: Cerathorn's three-horn configuration provides a reading of rock-seam depth when pressed to stone - miners in Granite Heights call this 'the triple test.'

About Titanoceros

Titanoceros is the mountain made flesh - its body covered in thick overlapping stone-plate armour, its three horns each large enough to be called a landmark, and its four legs wide as granite pillars. When it walks, small tremors precede it. When it stops, the stillness feels geological. Old Granite Heights legends say Titanoceros was carved by the mountains themselves as their answer to Abalont's shadow - something that could not be moved, could not be turned, and would simply still be there when everything else had changed.

Field note: The ground within thirty metres of a stationary Titanoceros shows measurably denser stone compression - as though the earth itself is consolidating around it.
