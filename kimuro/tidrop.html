About Tidrop

Tidrop is a small sky-blue sea serpent with a rounded head, tiny budding fins on its cheeks, and a short tail that swishes constantly through the air as though it is already swimming. Twin wisps of water-steam curl from its nostrils, leaving tiny droplets wherever it goes. It was found in a thermal pool at the lava crater's edge - the only creature that thrives where boiling water meets molten stone. Trainers say Tidrop's cheerful nature comes from never knowing a cold moment in its life.

Field note: Tidrop leaves a tiny trail of water-droplets wherever it travels, even in the hottest chambers of the lava crater.

About Hydrofen

Hydrofen has grown long and sinuous, with a proud crown-like crest of ice-blue spines along its head and a pair of broad webbed wings folded against its sides. Its coiling blue body gleams with frost patterns despite the surrounding heat, and its tail splits into a fan of fin-rays at the tip. It can swim through lava streams by surrounding itself with a shell of pressurized water, emerging on the other side entirely dry. The guardians of the lava crater valued its calm presence - a cold eye in a world of fire.

Field note: Hydrofen coils around thermal vents at night and absorbs the heat into pressurized water reserves used in battle.

About Typhdrake

Typhdrake is a massive coiling water dragon with a full crown of sweeping icy-blue horns, enormous bat-like wings edged in silver-blue, and a deep chest armored in overlapping glacial scales. Its body fills the widest corridors of the lava crater, and its passage creates a wall of cold mist that momentarily snuffs the nearest flames. When it speaks, its voice carries the crash of ocean waves against a cliff face. The ancient lore says Typhdrake weeps cold rain whenever a great word is forgotten, and the crater pools refill overnight.

Field note: Each of Typhdrake's horn-tips holds a frozen drop of ocean water from the day it was born - they never melt.
