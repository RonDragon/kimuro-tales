About Leafkin

Leafkin is a small, upright creature with a round pale body and large green leaf-ears that fan out wider than its head. Three leaf-tufts sprout from its crown, and a broad green leaf-tail sweeps behind it. Its bright green eyes glow softly in the dim light of the Dark Forest, making it easy to spot among the roots - which is how younger creatures find their way back to the path. When wind moves through the canopy, Leafkin's leaf-ears tilt like tiny radar dishes, reading the direction and weight of every gust.

Field note: Leafkin's three crown-tufts each bend toward a different wind direction; together they show the full wind-rose without a compass.

About Frondeer

Frondeer is a young stag whose body is armored with overlapping layers of green leaves and whose branching antlers each end in small leaf-clusters that whisper in the breeze. Yellow flower-dots mark its flanks like a second coat, and its eyes carry the focused calm of a creature that has memorized every hiding place in the Dark Forest. It moves through the densest undergrowth without a sound, leaf-antlers threading between branches as naturally as any twig. Travelers lost in the forest recognize Frondeer by the faint leaf-rustle that stays just ahead of them on the path home.

Field note: Frondeer sheds one leaf from its antler-clusters for every hour it spends in direct sunlight - a useful clock for forest travelers.

About Verdantler

Verdantler is a great leonine stag whose entire body is wrapped in a living coat of leaves, from the full-mane of layered fronds around its neck to the dense leaf-plating along its back and flanks. Its massive antlers branch high and wide, each tine carrying three or four leaf-clusters that catch even the faintest breeze and amplify it into a soft roar that fills the Dark Forest's canopy. The forest's oldest trees tilt toward it slightly when it passes - as though bowing to a guardian they have known for centuries.

Field note: When Verdantler shakes its mane, the leaves it sheds drift upward rather than down, rising until they reach the canopy and reattach to whatever branch needs them most.
