About Flickfawn

Flickfawn is a small sandy-furred foal with a bright mane of orange-gold flames rising from its neck and a single tail-flame that flickers with every breeze across the sand dunes. Its round amber eyes are warm and eager, and its four legs end in neat hooves that send up tiny puffs of hot sand with each step. When a gust crosses the dunes, its mane-flame stretches and gutters sideways and Flickfawn tilts its head to feel the full warmth of it, pausing whatever it was doing. The sand dunes children say Flickfawn is how fire learned to run.

Field note: Flickfawn's mane-flame stretches in the exact direction of the next wind gust, moments before it arrives - the best weather-sign in the dunes.

About Emberfur

Emberfur is a powerful dune lion with a full mane of blazing orange-gold flame-fur that rises and streams behind it as it sprints across the sand dunes at frightening speed. Its tawny coat is burnished gold-brown, and three separate flame-tails stream behind it like burning ribbons when it runs full-out. It hunts in the late afternoon when the dune wind picks up, using the thermal updrafts to leap farther than any creature its size should manage. Its footprints in the sand are small scorch-rings - the dune wanderers follow them like a map toward safety.

Field note: Emberfur's three flame-tails merge into one at top speed - it has never been caught by anyone counting to three.

About Pyremanx

Pyremanx is a vast, broad-shouldered lion with a mane of roaring flames that rises taller than its head and casts orange light across the sand dunes for a hundred meters. Four powerful flame-tails stream behind it, each thick as a torch, and when it lifts its head and roars, the hot wind it generates bends the dune crests and scatters burning sand in all directions. The sand dunes creatures stop and face it when it passes - not from fear, but recognition, the way a younger student stops when an ancient teacher enters. It never hurries. It has already arrived.

Field note: Pyremanx's roar generates a localized hot wind that flips the sand dunes into new shapes - cartographers must redraw their maps the morning after.
