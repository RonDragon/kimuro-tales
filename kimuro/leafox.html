About Leafox

Leafox is a small green fox-pup with large golden eyes and a bushy tail that ends in a cluster of rounded leaves. Its ears are lined with delicate leaf-veining, and small leaf-tufts grow along the top of its head. It zips through the Bug Thicket's undergrowth in short bursts, stopping to sniff each new plant with intense concentration before tearing off again. The light filtering through the thicket canopy catches the sheen of its leaf-markings and traces bright lines across the forest floor wherever it runs.

Field note: Leafox's leaf-tail turns a deeper green whenever rain is less than thirty minutes away, acting as a reliable weather warning for forest travelers.

About Verdfox

Verdfox has grown into a lean, confident fox with a rich green coat shot through with golden veins that glow when the Bug Thicket's light-beams reach it. Its tail has expanded into a full fan of overlapping green leaves, and it uses this fan to sweep trails clear of debris so younger creatures can follow safely. Gold circlets mark its legs where the light element has crystallized into living ornament. It knows the Bug Thicket's paths by a combination of scent and light-angle, navigating confidently even when fog cuts visibility to a meter.

Field note: Verdfox marks safe paths through the thicket by pressing its leaf-tail against moss-covered stones, leaving a faint bioluminescent stamp that lasts three days.

About Sylvamane

Sylvamane is a large, imposing fox with a flowing mane of living leaves that cascades from its head and shoulders like a cloak, glowing softly gold where the light filters through. Multiple leaf-tails stream behind it in a layered fan, and every step it takes leaves brief glowing footprints in the soil that fade after a few seconds. It walks the outer edge of the Bug Thicket each night, checking that every boundary-plant is healthy, and its golden eyes can read the health of a tree from thirty meters away. Guardians called it the Warden of Green Light.

Field note: Sylvamane sleeps standing up, eyes half-open, and the glow of its mane dims or brightens with the health of the forest around it.
