About Cobralet

Cobralet is a plump round-faced young serpent with smooth purple scales and large, dotted eyes that already hold an unusual depth of focus. It coils contentedly on marsh-lily pads, watching pond-skaters with patient, unblinking intensity. It hasn't yet learned to intimidate but practices spreading a tiny hood that immediately collapses again from its own weight. Explorers who spend time near the Poison Marsh say that Serplets seem to stare into the middle distance as though reading something only they can see - the first sign of their growing psychic sensitivity.

Field note: Cobralet's eyes track movement up to thirty seconds before it happens - it positions itself to watch a spot where a fly will land before the fly has left its previous perch.

About Vipyx

Vipyx rears up in a commanding cobra spread, its expanded purple hood edged with faint shimmering lines that ripple like heat-haze above marsh water. Purple-tinged vapor streams from its fanged mouth. Its psychic gaze is now strong enough to cause disorientation in anyone who meets it too long - creatures larger than itself avoid direct eye contact. It has claimed the deepest lily-pool channels of the Poison Marsh as its hunting ground, using psychic pulses to stun prey before it strikes. Guardians who studied it noted it seemed aware of them before they were aware of it.

Field note: Vipyx's hood shimmer can be seen from sixty metres in fog - marsh travellers have learned to use it as an 'avoid this path' marker.

About Cobraeth

Cobraeth towers in a full hood-spread that blots out the marsh sky above it, its scales deepened to blue-purple and its hood bearing iridescent psychic markings that pulse like slow heartbeats. The venom it exhales as mist can cloud a traveler's thoughts completely. Its eyes have become luminous, and those who look into them report seeing their own memories replayed with perfect clarity. It is the Poison Marsh's oldest living psychic, and the marsh plants grow in spiral patterns around every place it has rested - as though the land itself tries to read its mind.

Field note: The spiral growth patterns around Cobraeth's resting spots persist for decades after it has moved on, marked on every old map of the Poison Marsh.
