About Trunkit

Trunkit is a plump grey elephant calf with a short trunk, rounded ears, and a scatter of small glowing orange gem-spots on its hide like embers on grey stone. The gems are natural mineral deposits that form in the skin of Sand Dunes elephants at birth - they provide warmth in cool desert nights and light when Trunkit is in a completely dark environment. It uses its short trunk to probe the sand, locating buried moisture and mineral deposits with surprising accuracy for something born only recently.

Field note: Trunkit's gem-spots glow more brightly the deeper below the sand the nearest water source is - making it a living well-finder.

About Stonetusk

Stonetusk wears natural rock armor - thick plates of grey stone have formed over its shoulders, back, and head, leaving its legs and trunk relatively free. Its two small tusks have begun to emerge and curve slightly forward. Moving through the Sand Dunes, Stonetusk uses its weight to press down into the soft sand and feel vibrations from deep below - a kind of seismic listening that tells it where the dunes are stable and where they will shift. Caravans in the Sand Dunes follow Stonetusk paths because they are always the safest routes.

Field note: Every major caravan route across the Sand Dunes follows a path first walked by a Stonetusk - none of them have ever led into sinking sand.

About Terramorph

Terramorph has become a living geological event - its body is broad as a mesa, its back draped in a massive flowing mane of compressed earth and root-system that trails behind it across the dunes like a small moving hill. Its curved tusks are long and heavily ridged, and its hide beneath the mane is thick as castle stone. When Terramorph walks, the dunes ripple outward from its footprints in slow waves. The Sand Dunes themselves are said to be the fossilized footprints of Terrarmorphs so ancient they have since become part of the landscape.

Field note: A single Terramorph footprint takes approximately nine hundred years to fully fill back in with undisturbed sand.
