About Carapuff

Carapuff is a tiny segmented pill bug with a pale silver-grey shell and small stubby legs that barely reach the ground. When startled, it curls into a perfect silvery ball and rolls wherever gravity takes it - which is often directly into something else's feet. It lives in Bug Thicket's damp undergrowth, tucked beneath leaves and mossy logs, emerging at twilight to roll methodically over the same patch of ground it considers its territory. Young explorers keep them as companions because a content Carapuff purrs faintly in your pocket.

Field note: Carapuff rolls into a ball when startled and travels further than expected - one was found 200 metres from its home patch following a particularly loud noise.

About Carapax

Carapax has grown its segments wide and overlapping, giving its oval body the look of a living shield. Its legs have thickened and its rolling form is now genuinely dangerous - other creatures hear it rolling through the undergrowth and step aside quickly. It has a habit of unrolling in exactly the right spot to block a threat before its trainer has even spotted one. In Bug Thicket, Carapax is considered a reliable first warning of anything wrong on the forest floor.

Field note: Carapax unrolls with enough momentum to knock over a creature three times its size - trainers have started treating its unrolling as a useful battle signal.

About Titanshell

Titanshell is a living fortress - its shell so dense and wide that in ball-form it is nearly a metre across, and rolling, it leaves a flat track through undergrowth. Its segmented plates have hardened to something between insect chitin and folded iron, and the ridges on its back are sharp enough to be read like topographic lines. When it unrolls fully, the ring of its plates hitting the ground is heard across Bug Thicket. Old grove-keepers say Titanshell once rolled down a hill and stopped a flood barrier from breaking - the thicket has never flooded since.

Field note: Titanshell in ball-form is unmoved by anything short of a deliberate explosion - it has been recorded sitting in the path of a mudslide for six hours, unrolling only once it had passed.
