About Scythlet

Scythlet is a small dark-furred quadruped whose back is already bristling with short metal spikes, hard to the touch and cold even in summer. It lives in Shadow Caves' narrow passages where it can brush both walls at once, using the spikes to feel vibrations in the stone. Its eyes are adapted to complete darkness and reflect a faint silver in lamplight. It is wary but not aggressive, retreating at raised voices - the caves echo strangely and it treats every reverberation as a potential threat. Young ones tap their spikes against cave walls to communicate across the dark.

Field note: Scythlet can transmit information to others of its kind by tapping its spikes in specific rhythms against shared cave walls - cave researchers call it 'spike-talk.'

About Scythorn

Scythorn has grown heavy and broad, its coat dark as charcoal and its back spikes now long enough to scrape the cave ceilings. Each spike is individually sharp and has a slight backward curve, making them effective at catching anything that tries to grab it from above. It moves through Shadow Caves with a slow commanding authority, and the smaller creatures part before it without being told. When two Scythorn cross paths in a narrow tunnel, they compare spike-length by standing side by side for exactly thirty seconds before one yields the passage.

Field note: Scythorn's spike tips are self-sharpening - the points regrow within 48 hours after being broken, slightly sharper than before.

About Scythorax

Scythorax is a massive dark predator whose entire back and shoulders carry a forest of long steel spikes that rattle softly when it walks - a sound that carries through Shadow Caves' tunnels like a warning bell. Its coat is pure black, absorbing all light, and its eyes glow silver in the deepest dark. The spikes are long enough that Scythorax must turn sideways to fit through certain cave passages, and over centuries it has widened all of its regular routes to suit itself. The guardians' records note that Scythorax spikes were found embedded in the cave walls at heights that suggest it was fighting something immense - a story no one has fully told.

Field note: Scythorax's regular cave-routes are identifiable by parallel scratch-marks at shoulder height on both walls, left by its outer spike-tips for centuries.
