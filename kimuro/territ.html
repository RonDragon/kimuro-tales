About Territ

A plump, sandy-tan pig-shaped creature with a round diamond-shaped stone patch on its forehead, Territ roots through the amber desert searching for buried mineral seeds with its blunt little snout. It has short sturdy legs and a round belly, and when alarmed it hunches into a ball so its smooth stone-mark forehead faces outward. Territ is curious but cautious, often following larger creatures to nibble the pebbles they dislodge. The guardians of the Amber Desert keep Territ as a good-luck charm, believing it can sniff out hidden wells.

Field note: Territ rolls perfectly round pebbles into its sleeping hollow each night, arranging them in a small spiral before resting.

About Terosaur

Terosaur has grown long and low, its body now covered in interlocking brown-stone armour plates that fan into a jagged ridge down its spine. It moves with a slow deliberate lizard-swagger across the amber desert floor, leaving deep claw-scrapes in the hardpack. When threatened it puffs its neck plates outward, revealing a rattling pattern of loose rock scales that clatter together as a warning. Travellers report that Terosaur herds sometimes march single-file through narrow canyons before dawn, clattering like a procession of stone bells.

Field note: Terosaur's ridge-plates loosen in midday heat and rattle like gravel in a tin whenever it shakes its spine.

About Terratops

Dark stone-black and massively built, Terratops stands on two powerful hind legs with broad clawed forearms, its entire back and crown erupting in jagged volcanic-rock spikes that glow faintly amber at their tips. Flaming ember-orange eyes pierce through the desert haze, and the heat radiating off its spines makes the air above it shimmer. Ancient amber desert lore calls Terratops the Spine of the Desert - it is said that every major canyon was formed by one Terratops lying down to rest. When it roars, the spikes on its back vibrate, sending a crackling stone-grind echo down canyon walls.

Field note: Terratops's back-spikes heat to over 400 degrees at noon; hawks have been spotted using them as perches to dry their wings in winter.
