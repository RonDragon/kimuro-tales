About Prismite

Prismite is a tiny pale round-bodied moth-butterfly with oversized white wings marked by a single round eyespot on each. Its two long antennae curve outward like question marks, always quivering. It drifts through the crystal mines' lower chambers on barely-there wingbeats, drawn toward any facet of quartz that refracts light. When a crystal catches the mine-light and splits it into colors, Prismite freezes mid-air, antennae spread wide, absorbing the spectrum. Mine-workers who see a Prismite hovering in a dark passage say there is always a hidden gem-vein behind the wall at that exact point.

Field note: Prismite hovers motionless for exactly as many seconds as the number of gem-facets in the crystal it is studying.

About Luminyx

Luminyx has taken on a more otherworldly form - its body now a luminous blue-white orb hovering within a halo of diaphanous wings that blur with motion. Two long curved horns sweep back from its head, and trailing light-tendrils drift behind it wherever it moves. It barely touches the mine floor, preferring to hover at head height and study the faces of creatures it meets. When it focuses its large dark eyes on you, there is a sensation of being read - every memory you carry floating to the surface of your mind like bubbles. Luminyx is the living map of Crystal Mines' deepest echo chambers.

Field note: Looking into Luminyx's eyes for ten seconds causes any forgotten word to surface in your memory, clear and complete.

About Chromalith

Chromalith is breathtaking - a massive butterfly with wings that contain every color simultaneously, each cell a living prism that refracts light into shifting spectra. Its purple-and-gold body is jeweled with crystal nodules, and its long trailing hindwings sweep the ground like a ceremonial robe. In flight, it leaves a corridor of prismatic light that lasts for hours. The crystal mines go quiet when Chromalith enters: every gem, every facet, every crystal vein begins to pulse in sync with its wingbeat. The guardians say Chromalith is the mines' memory-keeper - that each color in its wings represents a different kind of knowing, and that it has never once forgotten a single thing since it hatched.

Field note: Every color shift in Chromalith's wings corresponds to a different mood of the mines - scholars have counted forty-seven distinct hues.
