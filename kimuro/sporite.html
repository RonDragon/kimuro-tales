About Sporite

Sporite is a tiny round mushroom creature with a smooth brown cap and big wide eyes that blink slowly and independently of each other. Its two stumpy legs shuffle it along the sand with surprising speed for something so round, and tiny spore puffs drift from its cap whenever it hops. Despite living in the Sand Dunes, Sporite thrives where underground moisture seeps up between the dunes, forming small oasis patches that only it can find. Young Sporites are born in clusters of three, and they spend their first weeks following each other in circles.

Field note: The spores Sporite releases each morning contain a faint sweet smell that attracts underground beetles - which then aerate the soil and help moisture travel upward.

About Myconaut

Myconaut stands upright now on two solid legs, its broad flat mushroom cap casting a wide shadow over its compact body. It has short sturdy arms it keeps crossed in front of its chest, giving it an air of permanent skepticism. The cap has widened and flattened, and it releases a visible cloud of spores when startled that can put small creatures to sleep for a few hours - a trick it uses to negotiate rather than fight. Myconaut has been spotted at ancient memory-stone sites throughout the Sand Dunes, seemingly reading the carvings.

Field note: Myconaut is always found within forty meters of an underground water source, even in the driest dune fields - it has never once been wrong.

About Fungolith

Fungolith is ancient beyond reckoning - more root system than creature now, with thick twisted root-legs that drive deep into the sand and draw water from aquifers no other being has reached. Its trunk-like body is scored with bark, and its massive mushroom cap shelters an entire ecosystem of smaller creatures beneath it. When it speaks, the vibration travels through the ground as much as through the air, and sand dunes shift slightly in response. The oldest oases in the dunes were not natural - they formed beneath a resting Fungolith.

Field note: Fungolith has never moved more than ten meters in recorded history - but the sand dunes around it have slowly reshaped over centuries to face it.
