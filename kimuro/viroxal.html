About Viroxal

Viroxal is a perfectly round green orb of a creature with a smooth body dotted with neat little green spines and wide friendly eyes that blink slowly from its center. Tiny thorn-nubs ring its equator like a crown. It rolls through the Bug Thicket's leaf litter with remarkable speed for its compact form, nudging against tree roots with soft bumps. Beetles and crawling insects treat it as a landmark - they navigate around it as they would a small rock, which Viroxal seems to enjoy enormously. It grows one new spine every full moon.

Field note: Viroxal's round body is rigid enough to hold its shape under the weight of a fully grown thorn-beetle standing on top - it doesn't even dent.

About Viroxian

Viroxian has shed the smooth orb of youth into a fierce-looking cluster of green crystalline spines that radiate outward in all directions. A narrow face with sharp eyes peers from between the longest spines at the front. The Bug Thicket's beetles now give it wider berth - a spine-brush leaves a sting that lasts for hours. It wedges itself into trunk-forks and hollow log openings as territory markers, and anything that blunders through dislodges it into a spinning, spiny defensive roll. It is growing something - the spines nearest its core are beginning to hint at the larger dragon-form to come.

Field note: Viroxian can control each spine independently; it raises them in precise sequential waves as a threat display - first-time watchers always count the pattern without meaning to.

About Viroxarch

Viroxarch sprawls low and vast, its entire surface a bristling landscape of massive green crystal-spines that dwarf any individual creature nearby. From above it resembles an island of thorns more than any living thing. Eyes that miss nothing sit deep among the front spines, tracking every insect and creature in its territory with unhurried precision. The Bug Thicket actually thrives under its coverage - the spines catch dew and channel it to the root-soil, and fallen outer spines enrich the ground with minerals. Trainers say it is the thicket's true gardener, not its predator.

Field note: The outer spines of Viroxarch shed naturally each season; thicket-floor mushroom rings grow in perfect circles exactly where the shed spines landed.
