About Vulpink

Vulpink is a small, vivid orange fox kit with a broad fluffy tail, oversized ears, and a tiny green gem set into its chest that glows faintly in desert shade. Green leaf-ruffles edge its neck like a little collar. Despite living in the amber desert, it is drawn to every scraggly desert shrub and dried herb-plant it can find, sniffing the desiccated leaves with intense interest and sometimes napping atop them. It carries warmth wherever it goes - the herbs it sleeps on smell faintly of cinnamon the morning after. Desert travelers look for the glowing chest-gem as a sign of water nearby.

Field note: The gem in Vulpink's chest glows brightest above underground water; desert scouts follow young Vulpinks to find hidden oasis springs.

About Vixbran

Vixbran has grown sleek and quick, its orange-amber coat now decorated with vivid leaf-shaped markings along its flanks and leafy fronds sprouting from its tail tip and ears. The green chest-gem has brightened. It runs the amber desert's inter-dune troughs at full sprint at dawn and dusk, leaving trails of tiny green shoots in the sand behind its paws that blossom briefly before the heat takes them. The Amber Desert's old stories say that wherever Vixbran runs in a full circle, a ring of flowers blooms for exactly one day.

Field note: Vixbran's pawprints leave soil-nutrient traces that accelerate seed germination - in tested samples, seeds from its trail sprout four times faster than those from untouched sand.

About Pyrocanth

Pyrocanth stands magnificent on a desert ridge, its body wrapped in living vines and blossoms that trail and glow in the amber sunset. Its tail fans into six broad leaf-fronds that catch the hot wind. The gem in its chest now blazes orange-green, and every step leaves a brief bloom of desert wildflowers that fade within seconds of its passing. In the Amber Desert's oldest legend, Pyrocanth arrived before the first drought and found a way to keep the desert flowering - not by ending the heat, but by carrying the seeds of everything beautiful in its coat and releasing them wherever the land was thirstiest.

Field note: The scent trail Pyrocanth leaves while running is strong enough to attract migratory beetles that pollinate desert plants - ecologists credit it with maintaining the desert's sparse bloom cycle.
