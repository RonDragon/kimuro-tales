About Scutiny

Scutiny carries a ridged dome shell on its back, reddish-brown with a surface texture like old hardwood - and it collects new shells obsessively, swapping out the one it has every few weeks for a slightly larger one it has been eyeing in Coral Bay's shallows. One large orange claw does most of its work, clicking at pebbles and prying apart shells to evaluate them. It makes a low bubbling noise while doing this - a sound the bay's fishers have come to associate with the presence of particularly good shelling spots.

Field note: Scutiny evaluates up to seven different shells before choosing a new one, and returns rejected shells to the exact spot it found them.

About Scutlash

Scutlash carries a large barrel-like shell reinforced with strips of steel-hard mineral deposits it has layered on over seasons of submersion in Coral Bay's mineral springs. Two bright orange claws reach past the shell rim, one larger for cracking open encrusted food and one smaller and more precise for intricate shell-work. It has begun decorating its shell with found objects - a knack that alarms only visitors to the bay, since locals are used to seeing a Scutlash with a pebble mosaic on its back. When threatened, it retreats fully inside and seals the opening shut with its large claw.

Field note: No two Scutlash shells carry the same decoration arrangement - each shell is considered a personal statement, accumulated over three to four years.

About Scutlord

Scutlord emerges from Coral Bay's deepest channel carrying a shell that has fused to layers of steel mineral deposits - its back is now an armored fortress of spined ridged plates that nothing short of a direct strike can dent. Its claws are massive and thick-armored, and when it walks the full weight of its shell sends vibrations through the bay floor that small fish interpret as a local earthquake. The guardians found old maps that named certain reef formations 'Scutlord Shelf' - suggesting the creature has been shaping Coral Bay's landscape for centuries.

Field note: Scutlord's fused shell has been documented absorbing direct blows from boulder-sized debris during storms without showing fracture.
