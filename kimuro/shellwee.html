About Shellwee

Shellwee is a tiny, bright-green land turtle with a smooth domed shell patterned in hexagonal tiles and two big round eyes that take up most of its face. Its four legs are stubby and it walks with a determined waddle. Bug Thicket's mossy floor suits it perfectly - it munches leaf-tips and dewdrops all morning, and marks the paths it has already walked by leaving tiny damp footprints. It follows the guardians' old learning-trails without knowing why, as if the paths remember it too.

Field note: Shellwee's footprints stay damp twice as long as dew on any other creature, making its trail easy to follow in dry thicket.

About Chelasaur

Chelasaur has grown a stronger, ridged shell of deeper green with golden-edged tiles, and its neck and legs have thickened considerably. It moves with slow confidence through the Bug Thicket underbrush, pausing to press its head flat against the earth and listen for the faint vibrations of root-songs growing below. When it finds one, it will wait patiently beside it until a young trainer passes - it can wait three days without eating if the word is important enough.

Field note: Chelasaur presses its chin flat to the ground for up to ten minutes at a time, listening for root vibrations no other creature can detect.

About Coralking

Coralking carries an entire living reef on its back - a crown of branching coral-like growths, orange and gold, that sprout from the edges of its massive shell like a natural fortress wall. Its broad shell is thick as stone, and the coral fringe waves gently even in still air. In the Bug Thicket, water seeps from beneath it wherever it rests, making small pools that other creatures gather around. The guardians believe it is the bridge between sea-songs and land-songs - the creature that carries both at once.

Field note: A Coralking resting in one spot for a week will cause a small freshwater spring to appear beneath it.
