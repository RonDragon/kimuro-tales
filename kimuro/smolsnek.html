About Smolsnek

Smolsnek is a small, round-headed orange-brown serpent with a tiny flame flickering at the end of its tail, which it curls protectively inward when resting. Its eyes are large and bright, and it sways in a slow S-curve when alert - a habit that desert traders find either charming or unsettling depending on their experience with fire serpents. It lives in the warm pockets between amber desert rock formations, coiling into tight spirals to sleep. Children who encounter one are surprised by how soft its scales feel, which is not a feature it appreciates being commented on.

Field note: Smolsnek's flame tail never flickers in wind but always flickers when it is being watched, as though showing off.

About Inferno

Inferno is a substantial orange-yellow serpent with a row of fire-spines and pointed frills erupting along the ridge of its body. It moves in long smooth waves that cross the desert floor faster than they appear, and the fire-frills glow brighter when it accelerates. In the amber desert it follows the subterranean heat channels beneath the surface, surfacing at points where the sand is hottest. Ancient maps mark these surfacing spots as landmarks - an Inferno will always appear at the same spot within two days of a desert sandstorm.

Field note: Inferno's spine-frills each heat to a different temperature, and the total pattern of hot and cool frills is unique to every individual.

About Infernoviper

Infernoviper is a legendary desert serpent so large its full body can circle a small oasis when it coils. Dramatic fire-wings of living flame fan from its mid-section, and its tail blazes with a permanent torch-flame that serves as a beacon for lost desert travelers. When it rises above the dune horizon the surrounding sand vitrifies into amber glass in a ring beneath it. The guardians of the amber desert believe Infernoviper is the desert's own memory - that it has witnessed every sandstorm, every voice raised in the waste, since the desert was formed.

Field note: Infernoviper's flame-wings never flap but they change orientation to indicate wind direction; desert pilots have used them as an instrument for generations.
