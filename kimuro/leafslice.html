About Leafslice

Built like a folded leaf on two legs, Leafslice is a vivid yellow-green, its flat body mimicking a large serrated leaf so perfectly that even experienced naturalists step past it unnoticed. Its triangular head bobs on a long neck, and its two forearms end in leaf-shaped blades it snaps together to make a crisp cutting sound. It hides on the big-leaf plants of the bug thicket, waiting until a child speaks a word out loud - then it snaps its blades in approval.

Field note: Leafslice snaps its forearms exactly twice for a word it already knows, and three times for a word it has never heard before.

About Bladewing

Bladewing has grown lean and angular, its forearms now long jagged blades of forest-green chitin that it holds crossed over its chest. Two swept-back wings, veined like giant leaves, let it burst upward in short flight. Its compound eyes catch every small movement. When it stalks through the thicket, it moves one precise leg at a time, perfectly silent. Old thicket guides say following a Bladewing's gaze always leads to something the forest is trying to hide.

Field note: Bladewing can hold perfectly still for forty minutes at a stretch - the only giveaway is a slow rhythmic twitch at the tip of each wing.

About Verdantis

Verdantis commands the canopy layer of the bug thicket. Its four great leaf-wings spread wide enough to cast a shadow over a clearing, and its paired forearm blades are edged in dark iridescent green-black. When it stands at full height, the silhouette looks exactly like a tree split by lightning - which is precisely how it hides in plain sight. The oldest thicket lore says Verdantis marks the boundary between bug-thicket territories by slicing a symbol into bark, and no creature crosses the mark.

Field note: Verdantis's territorial marks are always cut exactly 1.5 meters above the ground - perfectly eye-level for a standing guardian.
