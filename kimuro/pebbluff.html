About Pebbluff

Pebbluff is a compact dark-shelled tortoise whose hexagonal shell plates are already bolted-looking, as though assembled rather than grown. Its small legs carry it in a patient, unhurried trudge through the Amber Desert's hot sands. Each plate on its shell catches the desert sun slightly differently, giving its back a mosaic glint. It stops frequently to press its flat face to the ground and feel for the deep vibrations that tell it whether the sand ahead will bear its weight.

Field note: Pebbluff's shell plates expand slightly in heat and contract at night - the clicking sound at dawn in Amber Desert camps is usually a herd of Pebbluff adjusting.

About Caramite

Caramite is a solid, heavy tortoise whose dark shell has grown into thick interlocking iron-grey plates with orange-gold studs at each junction. Its head is broad and angular, and it pulls it low when it senses a threat - the shell closing flush to the ground creates a surface nearly impossible to overturn. It navigates the Amber Desert by the faintest tremor in the rock beneath the sand, finding ancient paths that no map captures. Even in sandstorms, Caramite continues without hesitation.

Field note: Caramite finds buried water sources by detecting the microscopic ground tremor that moving groundwater creates - desert survival guides recommend following one.

About Caramoth

Caramoth is a moving fortress - its shell is a fortress of dark iron-grey hexagonal plates studded with thick golden spikes at every corner, broad and heavy and ancient. Its legs are pillars. It moves exactly as slowly as it wishes and has never been observed moving faster, even in danger; it has simply never needed to. The Amber Desert's oldest dunes carry faint straight lines that geographers believe are the ancient walking routes of Caramoth, worn so deeply over millennia they became permanent features of the landscape.

Field note: Caramoth's shell spikes grow throughout its lifetime - the number of golden spikes on an adult can be used to calculate its age within about five years.
