About Chitling

Chitling is a pale, chubby insect larva - smooth white skin banded in faint rings, a rounded rear, and a dark head with two tiny black eyes and a small hooked beak-jaw. It squirms through Sand Dune tunnels just below the surface, leaving winding channels that explorers call echo-burrows, because the trails it makes seem to form a pattern when viewed from a high dune. It eats nothing but sand grains and the compressed sunlight locked inside them.

Field note: Chitling's burrow trails, viewed from the top of the nearest high dune, sometimes form a single clear spiral.

About Chitiderm

Chitiderm is entirely encased in a hard oval shell of burnished bronze-brown chitin, segmented in six horizontal bands. A faint orange glyph glows on its face-plate - the only sign of the creature resting inside. In the Sand Dunes it buries itself at the base of crescent dunes and waits, perfectly still, sometimes for weeks. Old sigils pressed into nearby sand tiles drift closer while it sleeps, as if drawn by the glow of its face-mark.

Field note: Sand tiles within one body-length of a buried Chitiderm rotate slowly toward it over three days, as if being read.

About Chitoptera

Chitoptera is a massive upright beetle-warrior: bronze-brown wing-cases folded across a wide thorax, thick raking foreclaws, huge curved horn above its brow, and two powerful hind legs that carry it in bounding rushes across the dune field. Its carapace has hardened over a thousand moults and now deflects wind-whipped sand entirely. It carries every old sigil from its earlier burrows in its memory, and guards the dune boundary as if it were the last memory-keeper between the desert and the dark.

Field note: Chitoptera can bound twelve body-lengths in one leap - its takeoff leaves a crater that fills with fine ripple-patterns.
