About Scutwee

Scutwee is a compact brown pangolin whose overlapping scales already show a faint amber glow at their centers - a hint of the lava energy it will come to carry. It wanders the Lava Crater's cooler outer rim, pressing its nose to the ground and tasting volcanic minerals with a long sticky tongue. The amber glow on its chest pulses faster when it is excited, and crater-children use this as a sort of mood-light, watching it brighten when they offer it a handful of mineral dust. It is gentle and shy, preferring to roll into a tight ball rather than engage.

Field note: Scutwee's amber chest-glow brightens by a measurable degree within one metre of an active volcanic vent - researchers use it as a heat sensor.

About Scuthorn

Scuthorn stands upright on two broad legs, its body thick with overlapping scales that have taken on a deep brown-orange hue from years of volcanic mineral absorption. An amber gem-like spot glows visibly on its chest, warm enough to act as a small heat source on cold crater nights. It uses its short powerful arms to climb the rough inner walls of Lava Crater, pressing each scale individually against the rock face to test heat tolerance before proceeding. It marks safe climbing routes by scoring its claws into the rock - a permanent guide for anything smaller that follows.

Field note: Scuthorn's amber chest-gem has been measured at 80 degrees Celsius during peak activity - hot enough to fry a small egg placed against it.

About Scutossus

Scutossus is a great dragon-like pangolin, its scales now layered so thickly they jut outward in overlapping rows like roof tiles forged from volcanic stone. The amber glow has spread from its chest across the grooves between every scale, lighting its body in warm amber from within. When it moves, the scales grind against each other with a sound like boulders settling, and the heat it radiates is enough to keep a wide circle of the crater floor permanently dry and warm. The guardians placed it in their most ancient fire-carvings, believing Scutossus was there when the crater first opened.

Field note: The ambient temperature five metres from a resting Scutossus runs 35 degrees higher than surrounding crater air - its body warms the stone it rests on permanently.
