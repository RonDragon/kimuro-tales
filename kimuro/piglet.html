About Piglet

Piglet is a compact brown boar-pup covered in coarse wiry bristles that stand up along its spine like a tiny hedgehog trying to pass as a boar. It snuffles through the lower slopes of Granite Heights with its flat snout pressed to the stone, listening for hollow cavities and cracking rock. Piglet always runs into obstacles slightly too fast, as though surprised by their continued existence, then huffs, backs up, and runs into them again with increased conviction. The Heights' stone-masons have noticed that places Piglet butts against repeatedly tend to crumble eventually - a slow but reliable demolition service.

Field note: Piglet has demolished more granite outcrops by repeated insistent headbutting than any tool in the Heights' masonry yard.

About Briskhog

Briskhog has broadened into a powerful boar with a dramatic crest of long pointed bristle-spines running from crown to rump, giving it a silhouette like a walking mountain ridge. Its tusks are now properly intimidating, and it uses them to lever boulders that would take three people to move. Briskhog is dogged in its habits: it takes the same paths daily and responds to any change in its route with visible displeasure, marking the problem obstacle with a deep tusk-gouge before working out how to deal with it. The Granite Heights' goat-herders follow its worn paths as the safest trails.

Field note: Briskhog's daily patrol paths have been used as the Granite Heights' official trail maps for longer than any surveyor has been practicing.

About Ironhusk

Ironhusk is a massively armored boar-titan whose entire body is plated in fused stone-and-hide panels that ring when struck as though cast in iron. The bristle-spines have become rigid stone-ridges, and its tusks are each as long as a young tree. Ironhusk moves across the Granite Heights like a slow landslide - inevitable and patient. The guardians' texts describe it as the Heights' Foundation: a creature who drove itself so deep into the granite that the mountain could not fall while Ironhusk stood within it, holding the heights steady against Abalont's tremors.

Field note: Ironhusk's footprints in granite measure two centimeters deep - geologists use them as pressure benchmarks for the region's rock surveys.
