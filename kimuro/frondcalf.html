About Frondcalf

Frondcalf is a round, compact creature that looks like a small antelope puppy with a cluster of fresh green leaves growing from the top of its head and a short tail tipped in leaf. Its warm brown coat has a faint scale-like shimmer, and the leaf-sprout on its crown bobs cheerfully with each step. At Dragon Spire's lower slopes where the warm-air thermals keep a surprising strip of green grass, Frondcalf grazes with the focused attention of something that knows it is in a dangerous neighborhood and means to thrive anyway.

Field note: Frondcalf's leaf-crown doubles in size between dawn and dusk on days when a dragon-thermal is expected, acting as a natural warning flag.

About Frondhorn

Frondhorn has grown into a formidable quadruped with a brown-and-green armor hide and multiple rows of stiff leaf-spines running along its back. Its four legs are thick and powerful, built for negotiating Dragon Spire's steep obsidian paths, and its now-prominent shoulder spines make it look wider than it is tall. When dragon-fire sweeps down the slope, Frondhorn simply lowers its head and holds - its leaf-spines absorb the heat and release it as a warm, dry wind from its flanks that actually fertilizes the sparse grass around it.

Field note: Frondhorn's back-spines store enough heat from a dragon-fire pass to warm a small camp for a full night, if you don't mind the faint smell of scorched leaves.

About Frondakhan

Frondakhan is an enormous, low-slung armored creature whose back is a fortress of interlocking leaf-armor plates and whose great spiked tail sweeps the path behind it clean of loose stone. Rings of overlapping leaf-spines cover its flanks and neck, and the whole body has deepened to a dark green-brown that makes it nearly invisible against Dragon Spire's vegetation. When a dragon passes overhead, Frondakhan does not flinch - it watches, and the dragon veers aside. This is the creature that taught the Spire's inhabitants that the most powerful thing on a mountain is not always the one that breathes fire.

Field note: Frondakhan's tail-sweep, performed once a day, clears enough loose stone from the spire-paths to prevent three separate rockfalls that would otherwise occur.
