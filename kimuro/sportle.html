About Sportle

Sportle is the most endearing creature in the crystal mines - a small round turtle with a mushroom cap growing from the top of its shell instead of the usual dome. The cap is pale green-brown and perfectly smooth, and the turtle beneath is big-eyed and wide-footed, ambling through the mine tunnels with patient good humour. The mushroom cap absorbs trace minerals from the cave air and channels them into the shell below, slowly hardening it from the inside. Sportle is the only creature that actually grows stronger the longer it stays underground.

Field note: Sportle's shell is 12% denser for every year spent in the crystal mines - the oldest ones ring like struck stone.

About Fungurtle

Fungurtle has grown into a heavy, deliberate plodder. Its shell is now distinctly ridged and mineral-dark, and the mushroom cap on top has broadened and begun to develop secondary cap growths around its edge. Crystal dust clings to the cap's gills and catches the mine's light. Fungurtle moves through the narrowest mine passages without hurry, occasionally stopping to press its cap against a crystal vein for several minutes - absorbing something, researchers believe, though what it takes from the crystal has never been identified.

Field note: Fungurtle sections absorb trace crystal resonance so precisely that placing a Fungurtle near a cracked crystal vein causes the crack to slowly anneal over weeks.

About Mycolemoth

Mycolemoth is an ancient, ponderous form - a great tortoise body whose shell has partially merged with the stone floor of the mines, ringed by a cluster of mushroom caps that have hardened to the texture of limestone. It moves so rarely that crystals have begun to grow directly on its shell, and the mushroom cluster on its back is large enough for small creatures to shelter under. The crystal mines were dug around Mycolemoth, not before it - it was there when the first pickaxe struck, and it was not impressed, but it allowed the miners to stay.

Field note: The crystal specimens growing on Mycolemoth's back are 40% more pure than any found in the walls - the mycelium filters the mineral as it grows.
