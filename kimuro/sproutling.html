About Sproutling

Sproutling is a small bright-eyed deer fawn with grass-green coat and a pair of leaf-shaped antler nubs that have just pushed through its forehead. Small fresh leaves sprout directly from the antler tips. It stands on slender legs at the edge of the sand dunes where desert meets the last line of grass - an unlikely place for a fawn, but Sproutling has found that the sand stays warm at its hooves long after the air cools. When it dips its head, the leaf antlers sweep the sand gently, and seeds fall from them.

Field note: Sproutling has never been seen eating - it absorbs sunlight through its leaf antlers at dawn for two minutes and seems satisfied.

About Bramlock

Bramlock stands at adult height with a full spreading rack of antlers thick with living leaves and a few small flowers. Its body has deepened to a rich tawny-green, with the legs patterned in climbing vine shapes. It walks the sand dune ridges at dawn, and where it steps, short grass pushes through the sand within the hour. It has been observed touching its antler tines to dune crests, and those dunes shift more slowly - as though roots have found them.

Field note: Bramlock's hoof-marks are found far inside the deepest sand dunes - places where no grass grows and no trail leads in.

About Verdanthos

Verdanthos is vast and unhurried - a great stag with a full crown of branching antlers so heavy with leaves, vines, and small blossom clusters that birds nest in the upper tines. Its powerful body is dark earth-brown, and its hooves sink deep even in packed sand, leaving prints filled immediately by green shoots. It moves rarely through the sand dunes, but each time it crosses the same ground, the dune that was there before is gone and a flat green plateau takes its place. The desert remembers where it has been.

Field note: A single fallen leaf from Verdanthos's antlers, pressed between two stones at sunrise, will root and grow in the driest sand.
