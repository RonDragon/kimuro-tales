About Tigrkit

A golden-yellow kitten with bright green eyes and a coat patterned in warm amber spots, Tigrkit pounces across the Storm Spire's rocky ledges with reckless joy. It is small enough to shelter in rock crevices between storm-bouts and has learned exactly which crevices have the best airflow. Its spots are faintly warm to the touch - early signs of the electrical charge it will carry as an adult. Tigrkit loves to swat at the small static discharges that crackle off the Spire's metal fixtures, and the sparks make its fur stand on end in a spectacular ruffle it appears proud of.

Field note: Tigrkit's spot-markings warm measurably when a lightning storm is within five kilometres - Storm Spire apprentices keep them as personal weather stations.

About Tigrawn

Tigrawn has grown into a lean, striking tiger-form - golden amber stripes on a tawny coat, with a dramatic crest of stone-grey back-spines that stand erect when it is charged. Its green eyes now hold a steady focused intensity, and its paws leave faint scorch-marks on the Storm Spire's stonework. It hunts through vertical terrain with startling comfort - its claws have small electrical grippers that let it cling to rock faces at angles that should send it sliding. Storm apprentices at the Spire track Tigrawn's preferred hunting routes because they reliably follow the most electrically active fault lines.

Field note: Tigrawn's preferred patrol routes trace the Storm Spire's most electrically active fault lines with consistent accuracy - it is used as a live geological sensor.

About Tigracron

Tigracron is a magnificent, powerful tiger-sovereign with a full mane of crackling stone-spine ridges across its shoulders and back, its golden coat now streaked with permanent electric-burn scars that glow faintly in low light. Its tail sweeps in a controlled arc that leaves a trailing arc of static in the air. When Tigracron roars at the Storm Spire's summit, the lightning rods mounted there discharge simultaneously - the engineers learned to budget for replacements. Ancient Spire texts describe Tigracron as the reason the Spire was built where it was: it had already chosen the site as the highest natural electrical ground for a hundred kilometres.

Field note: Tigracron's roar has been measured to cause simultaneous discharge from all eight of the Storm Spire's summit lightning rods - engineers replaced them quarterly before learning to work around its schedule.
